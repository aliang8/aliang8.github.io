Introduction

In this lesson you will learn another adverb that is hard to translate into English: 어쩐지. It is used in two types of situations. Are those situations really different, or is that just how our English minds see them?

1

어쩐지 – One doesn’t know the reason and/or only now realizes

어쩐지 is another peculiar Korean adverb. It is used in a specific style of sentence and carries a very specific nuance. You have met several adverbs like this, including 하필 in the previous lesson, and they are hard to translate perfectly. To get a feel for how 어쩐지 is used and translated, look at two situations.

Situation 1:

Imagine your friend wants to go to a neighborhood near downtown late at night. It is not the nicest part of town. You have never been there at night, but your gut says you probably should not go. You cannot give a specific reason; something about that place, especially at night, just feels off. You might tell your friend:

거기에 밤에 가는 게 불안해요
I don’t feel comfortable going to that place at night

That sentence is possible and natural, but in both English and Korean it does not fully convey the feeling. You would more likely say something like:

“There’s something about going to that place at night. I don’t know. Somehow, my gut is telling me that we shouldn’t go. I can’t explain it.”

In Korean, you can express all of that extra feeling with 어쩐지. For example:

거기에 밤에 가는 게 어쩐지 불안해요
There’s something about going to that place at night. I don’t know. Somehow, my gut is telling me that we shouldn’t go. I can’t explain it

어쩐지 is used in this type of sentence, where the speaker thinks or feels something but does not know why.

Situation 2:

Imagine you see your friend during the day and he seems much sadder than usual. He is normally positive and happy, so you cannot understand it. That night, somebody tells you his cat ran away in the morning. You could say something like:

그래서 슬퍼 보였어요
That’s why he looked sad

That sentence is possible and natural, but in both English and Korean it does not fully convey the feeling. You would more likely say something like:

“Oh, I didn’t know that! I was wondering why he looked so sad! That explains it!”

In Korean, you can express all of that extra nuance with 어쩐지. For example:

어쩐지 슬퍼 보였어요
Oh, I didn’t know that! I was wondering why he looked so sad! That explains it!

You could add more context to the situation, but it is not necessary:

고양이가 아침에 도망 나갔다고 들었어요! 그래서 그를 아침에 봤을 때 어쩐지 슬퍼 보였어요
Oh! I just heard his cat ran away this morning! I was wondering why he looked so sad! That explains it!

어쩐지 is used in this type of sentence, where newly discovered information lets the speaker make sense of something that could not be understood before.

—————–

These two uses confuse many learners. How can one word have two seemingly opposite meanings? In one type of sentence, the speaker does not know the reason for something. In the other, the speaker does know it.

The answer: the Korean definition of the word does not distinguish between the two situations. 어쩐지 shows that one thinks or feels something without knowing why (situation 1). If the speaker later discovers the reason (situation 2), 어쩐지 adds the feeling that the reason was unknown until just now.

How 어쩐지 is translated, then, depends on whether the reason is known or unknown. For example, “어쩐지 슬퍼 보였어요” could translate to:

= He looked sad for some reason. I don’t know. Somehow, my gut is telling me that he looked off.
– In the situation that you are looking at your friend and see him looking sad, but you don’t know why

= Oh, I didn’t know that! I was wondering why he looked so sad! That explains it!
– In the situation that you just found out why your friend is sad

Words like this are hard to capture in one or two English words. In a pinch, though, the following translations usually work:

어쩐지 슬퍼 보였어요
Somehow, he looks sad (in the situation where the speaker doesn’t know the reason he is sad), or

= No wonder he looked sad (in the situation where the speaker just found out the reason why he is sad)

Here are many example sentences, separated into two sections: one where we assume the speaker does not know the reason, and one where we assume the speaker has just found it out. The split is only there to show how the use and the translation depend on context. In real life, you rely on context to tell which situation you are dealing with.

Examples like Situation 2: The speaker just found out the reason for something

Used this way, sentences are often short and simply state what the recent discovery made the speaker realize; the reason or context is usually left out and only the result is given. Each example below has two sentences. The first, more common version gives no reason or context; the second adds it. Both are natural, but the first is the more common way to use 어쩐지.
