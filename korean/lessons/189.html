Introduction

In this lesson you will continue with ~(으)나 and learn two more of its meanings.

1

~(으)나: Irrelevance Among Options

In the previous lesson you saw many uses of ~나 and the purpose of ~(으)나. This lesson introduces more uses of ~(으)나.

In this usage, ~(으)나 usually attaches to two words (verbs, adjectives or 이다) in one sentence. It shows that the outcome does not change no matter which option happens. A simple example:

집에서 쉬나 공원에서 쉬나 피로는 풀리지 않아요
Whether I rest at home or in the park, the fatigue doesn’t go away

The speaker is not saying they must choose between resting at home and going to the park. Whether they go to the park or stay home, the situation is the same. That is the key to this use of ~(으)나: it does not matter which of the options is chosen.

This is similar to ~거나, introduced in Lesson 58. ~거나 is more about options that need to be chosen. For example, the first ~거나 example in Lesson 58 is:

여자 친구를 위해 편지를 쓰거나 선물을 사 줄 거예요
I will write a letter, or buy a present for my girlfriend

~거나 here means the speaker will either write a letter or buy a present; they will pick one, with a sense of choosing the best option, whatever that is. ~(으)나, by contrast, is for when there is no difference in what happens. A different sentence ending makes that clearer. For example:

여자 친구를 위해 편지를 쓰나 선물을 사 주나 마음은 같아요
Whether I write a letter or buy her a present, my heart is the same

With ~(으)나, the speaker is not saying they need to choose. Both options are viable, and the ending says it does not matter which one happens.

This use of ~(으)나 is more likely in formal writing, presentations, or analytical commentary than in casual everyday speech.
