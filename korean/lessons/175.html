Introduction

In this lesson you will learn how ~치고 shows that there are no exceptions within a group, and how adding ~는 to ~치고 shifts the nuance slightly.

1

~치고: No Exceptions within a Group

Attach ~치고 to a noun to say that every single one of those nouns, without exception, does a particular action or is a particular way. Let’s start a sentence with:

한국 사람치고…

You would finish this with an action or description that is true for all Korean people. Something every Korean person does. For example:

한국 사람치고 애국가를 모르는 사람이 없어요
Among Korean people, there isn’t one who doesn’t know the national anthem

Notice that the sentence ends negatively. 치고 typically needs a negative ending such as 없다 or 모르다. Although it looks like it would mean the same thing, this sentence would be unnatural:

한국 사람치고 애국가를 다 알아요

Another sentence start:

초등학생치고는…

To finish this, think of something true for all elementary school students, without exception. How about playing?

초등학생치고 놀이터에서 노는 것을 안 좋아하는 애는 없어요
Among elementary school students, there isn’t one who doesn’t like playing in the playground

Below are many more examples:

이 분야의 학자치고 하루에 열 시간 이상 공부하지 않는 사람이 없어요
Among scholars in this field, there’s not a single one who doesn’t study over 10 hours a day
연중무휴 가게치고 직원이 힘들어하지 않는 곳은 없어요
Among stores that are open year-round with no holidays, there isn’t one where the staff aren’t struggling
극지방 탐사치고 위험하지 않은 경우는 없어요
Among polar expeditions, there’s no such thing as one that isn’t dangerous
그 배우가 나온 드라마치고 성공하지 않은 작품이 없어요
Among the dramas that actor is in, there isn’t one that isn’t successful
동물을 학대하는 사람치고 다른 생명에 대해 존중심이 있는 사람이 없어요
Among those who abuse animals, there’s not one who has a sense of respect life
위험 물질을 수송하는 차량치고 사고 위험이 없는 것은 없어요
Vehicles transporting hazardous materials are never without risk of accidents
여태껏 그렇게 노력한 사람치고 좋은 결과를 못 본 사람이 없어요

Now let’s see what happens when ~는 is attached to ~치고.

2

치고는: For a…

You have seen ~은/는 added to many words and grammatical principles to give a comparison meaning. It has come up since the beginning, and was the main focus of Lesson 2, Lesson 17 and Lesson 104. Adding ~는 to ~치고 puts that meaning onto ~치고, letting you point out one stand-out from the group ~치고 is attached to. In a sense, this is the opposite of what we saw above. For example:

한국 사람치고는…

You would finish this by describing one stand-out Korean person, compared with the rest of Korean people. For example:

한국 사람치고는 미국 애국가를 잘 알아요
For a Korean person, they know the American national anthem well

Most Korean people wouldn’t know the American national anthem. If one does, you could say that “for a Korean person,” he knows it well. This compares the one person with the rest of the group. ~치고 lets you point out one stand-out.

Back to the elementary school example:

초등학생치고는…

To finish this, think of something that is not true for most elementary school students, but is true for this one stand-out student. For example:

초등학생치고는 그 학생이 아주 성숙해요
For an elementary school student that student is very mature

Below are many more examples:

학자치고는 유머 감각이 뛰어난 편이에요
