Introduction

In this lesson you will learn how to end a sentence colloquially with 말, and when that is natural.

1

~ㄴ/은/는/을/ㄹ 말이다: Stressing or Checking What was Already Said

말 is a versatile noun that can mean a word, words, or anything spoken. For example:

저는 아직 할 말이 있어요
I still have something to say
선생님이 아까 했던 말은 너무한 것 같아요
The thing (words) that the teacher said before was too much

You will often see 말 at the end of a sentence, where it stresses something that has already been said. For example:

그렇게 해야 한다는 말이야!

Let’s break this down. The whole phrase before 말 is something that was already spoken, and the speaker is now repeating it. ~ㄴ/은/는/을/ㄹ is attached to the clause so it can describe 말, just as quoted phrases describe the noun 것 in Lesson 52. Finally, 이다 is added to 말 to finish the sentence. The result: the speaker repeats what was said and stresses its importance.

In English, this is close to “you know,” or “What I’m saying is!”:

그렇게 해야 한다는 말이야!
What I’m saying is, it has to be done this way!

Formal examples are tricky here. Example sentences in these lessons usually use 저 and end in ~요, but using 말 this way is a bit like thrusting your opinion at the listener, so it can sound rude unless done gently. You can conjugate the 이다 attached to 말 formally, but be careful when you do, and keep your tone and intonation soft.

Because of that, here are some informal examples:

우등생이 되려면 항상 최선을 다해야 한다는 말이야
What I mean is, you have to always do your best to become an honor roll student
그 학생은 우등상을 받을 만하다는 말이야!
What I’m saying is, that student deserves to win the honor prize!
부활절에 가족과 함께 시간을 보내는 게 중요하다는 말이야
What I mean is, it’s important to spend time with your family on Easter
이 도표를 보고 결과를 쉽게 알 수 있다는 말이야
What I’m saying is, you can easily understand the result by looking at this diagram
이 문장은 물음표를 써야 한다는 말이야
What I mean is, you need to use a question mark at the end of this sentence
그건 정말 멋진 일이니까 느낌표를 써야 한다는 말이야!
What I’m saying is, since that’s really amazing, you should use an exclamation mark!
그 전통이 다시 부활했다는 말이야
What I’m saying is, that tradition has been revived
그 그림을 벽에 달았다는 말이야
What I mean is, I hung that picture on the wall

You have spent a lot of time recently on ~느냐. You can attach ~는 to ~느냐 to describe 말, as you learned in Lesson 161. For example:

진짜 이게 맞느냐는 말이야
I’m asking if this is really correct
도대체 왜 그렇게 행동하느냐는 말이야
I’m asking why on earth you’re acting like that
시험 보기 전에 왜 그렇게 초조해하느냐는 말이야
I’m asking why you’re so anxious before the exam

The quoted sentence can be a question, but it can also be an order. You learned to quote orders in Lesson 54 and saw the shortened form in Lesson 145. So you can attach ~(으)란 or ~(으)라는 to verbs to describe 말. For example:

빨리 결정하고 행동하라는 말이야
I’m telling you to decide quickly and take action
그냥 내가 시키는 대로 하라는 말이야
I’m telling you to just do as I say

In the same way, you can describe 말 with ~자 (from Lesson 44 and Lesson 53) by attaching ~는 to ~자. This repeats a quote where someone suggested doing an action together. For example:

그러니까 네 말은 지금 당장 가자는 말이야?
So you’re saying we should go right now?
결국 우리한테 일을 다 떠넘기자는 말이야?
So you’re saying we should take on all the work?
