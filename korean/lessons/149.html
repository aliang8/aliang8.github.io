Introduction

In this lesson you will learn about the word 하필: what it means, whether it even has a translation, and what kind of situation it belongs in.

1

하필 – Of all possible situations to occur, the most unfortunate one occurs

In earlier lessons you learned several adverbs that add nuance and feeling to a specific style of sentence. For example:

In Lesson 43, you learned how 만약 is used in hypothetical “if” sentences:

만약 집들이를 그날 했으면 제가 갈 수 있었을 거예요
If they did the house warming party on that day, I could have gone

In Lesson 59, you learned how 어쩌면 is used in sentences that express disbelief:

어쩌면 그렇게 하루 만에 마늘이 많이 썩을 수가 있어요?
How is it possible that the garlic can rot only after being in the sun for one day?

In Lesson 59, you learned how 아무래도 is used in uncertain “probably” sentences:

아무래도 제과점이 이미 문을 닫았을 것 같아요
The bakery is probably already closed

In Lesson 67, you learned how 마치 is used in sentences where the speaker adds imagery to a situation:

마치 그는 죽은 듯이 바닥에 누워 있었어요
He lay on the floor as if he were dead

Korean has many words like these with no direct English translation. Explaining them takes a lot of words to describe the nuance they carry.

하필 is another of these adverbs. It is used in a very specific style of sentence and carries a very specific nuance, so like the four words above it is hard to translate perfectly. To get a feel for it, look at a situation where it fits.

Imagine you are waiting for a package. You waited all morning and it has not arrived. You decide it is safe to pop out to the store for milk, since it will only take five minutes. While you are buying the milk, you get a text saying the package arrived, but nobody was home to sign for it, so it was not delivered. Looking at your phone, you might mutter:

택배가 지금 왔어?
The package arrived now?

That sentence is possible and natural, but in both English and Korean it does not fully convey the feeling of the moment. You would more likely say something like:

“The package arrived now? Of all times for me to go out to the store. Of all times for the package to arrive, it had to be right now?!”

In Korean, you can express all of that extra frustration with 하필. For example:

하필 택배가 지금 왔어?
The package arrived now? Of all times for me to go out to the store. Of all times for the package to arrive, it had to be right now?!

하필 is often used at the beginning of a sentence, but it does not have to be.

  • 택배가 하필 지금 왔어? Natural and acceptable
  • 택배가 지금 하필 왔어? Not the most natural way to say it, but still acceptable

하필 does not really translate to anything. Even Korean speakers with excellent English will struggle to give you an English word for it. It is simply used in a specific type of situation like the one above: many things could have happened, and the absolute worst of them did. You could translate it as unfortunately, but that does not quite do it justice.

Here are more scenarios.

Imagine you are carrying a big bag of groceries. Everything in it is wrapped in paper and plastic and essentially unbreakable, except one thing: a carton of eggs. As you bring the bag inside, you slip and just the eggs fall out. Of all the things that could have fallen, why the eggs? You could say something like:

계란만 떨어졌어!
Just the eggs fell out!

But this sentence does not fully convey the feeling. Instead, you would want to say:

하필 계란만 떨어졌어!
Of all things to fall out, only the eggs fell!

Most example sentences in these lessons use high respect honorifics. They can absolutely be used with 하필, but these sentences are often a kind of “self-speech,” so the examples in this lesson all use low respect honorifics. When speaking to somebody who deserves high respect, use the high respect forms.

 —–

Imagine you are an excellent student who always does the homework and is always prepared for class. One day your teacher assigns a few pages of the textbook, and for the first time ever you forget to read them. The next day there is a surprise test on exactly that content. You could say something like:

숙제를 처음으로 안 했는데 하필 오늘 시험을 봐야 돼
Of all days to have a test, it’s the day that I don’t do the homework for the first time

—–

Imagine you are taking a university class and do not know who the professor will be. Nine professors are great and everybody likes them; one is mean and nobody wants. On the first day, the door opens and the mean professor walks in. You could say something like:

하필 우리 교수님이 그 사람이야?
Of all professors to have, our professor is that person?

—–

Imagine you have waited months for one special day at an amusement park with all your friends. That morning, you wake up sick. You could say something like:

오늘은 놀이공원에 가는 날인데 하필 감기에 걸린 것처럼 몸이 안 좋아
Of all days for me to feel like I have a cold, it happens on the day that we’re going to the amusement park

—–

Imagine you never get lost, but you always bring a compass when hiking. The one day you forget it, you get lost. You could say something like:

하필 이번에 나침반을 안 챙겼네
Of all times for me to forget my compass, it had to be this time!

—–

Imagine you go to the mall to sell some old books at a used book store. Every store is open except the used book store. You could say something like:

하필 오늘 그 헌책방만 문을 닫았어
Of all things to be closed, it had to be the used book store

—–

As you can see, the best English translation of 하필 is usually something like “Of all…” Still, 하필 does not translate well, because of all means nothing without context.
