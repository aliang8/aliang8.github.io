Introduction

In this lesson you will learn ~(으)렴, a much softer way to make a command. It is used mostly by the older generation.

1

~(으)렴: Gentle Command

In Lesson 40 you learned to make commands with ~아/어(요) or ~(으)세요. Those are the most common ways. Another is ~(으)렴. For example:

밥을 많이 먹으렴
Eat a lot (of rice)

In the previous lesson you saw that ending a sentence with ~ㄴ/는단다 sounds gentle and motherly. ~(으)렴 is the gentle, motherly way to give a command. Older people (parents, grandparents) use it when talking to younger people. Think of it as a soft command, closer to a suggestion or gentle encouragement.

사탕 먹었으면 이빨 닦으렴
If you had candy, brush your teeth
저상버스가 오면 탈 준비하렴
When the low-floor bus arrives, get ready to board
용돈 생기면 조금씩 저금하렴
When you get an allowance, save a little bit each time
옷을 표백할 땐 장갑 꼭 끼고 하렴
When you bleach clothes, make sure to wear gloves
비상구를 막지 말고 항상 비워 두렴
Don’t block the emergency exit, always keep it clear
입덧이 심하면 무리하지 말고 푹 쉬렴
If your morning sickness is severe, don’t overdo it and get some rest
공황이 올 것 같으면 숨을 천천히 쉬렴
If you feel a panic attack coming, breathe slowly, sweetheart
이제 너도 컸으니 자율적으로 행동하렴

친구랑 언쟁하지 말고 사이좋게 지내렴

늦게까지 외박하지 말고 일찍 들어오렴

You will often see this in children’s books, especially when a parent is talking to a child. In the previous lesson you saw a short sentence from Hansel and Gretel with ~ㄴ/는단다. The sentence that follows the one from Lesson 181 contains ~(으)렴. Here are both sentences together:

너희들을 숲에 버리고 온 뒤 한순간도 행복하지 않았단다. 나를 용서해 주렴.
After abandoning you in the forest, I haven’t been happy for even a moment, my dear. Please forgive me.
