Introduction

In this lesson you will learn to attach ~ㄹ/을수록 to the end of a clause. It is a fairly advanced grammatical principle, but an easy one to grasp.

1

As One Situation Changes, Another Situation Changes:~ㄹ/을수록

~ㄹ/을수록 attaches to verbs, adjectives and 이다 to indicate that as one situation changes to some degree, another situation is affected and changes to that degree too. Here is a simple sentence to show the meaning:

공부를 할수록 똑똑해요

Here ~ㄹ/을수록 is attached to 공부하다. The speaker indicates that how much one studies affects how smart one is. A common English translation of ~ㄹ/을수록 is “As….” For example:

공부를 할수록 똑똑해요
As you study, you get smart

Implied in ~ㄹ/을수록 is the idea that if one studies to a certain degree, one gets smart to that degree, and if one studies even more, one gets even smarter. So it may be better to translate the sentence above in either of these ways:

공부를 할수록 똑똑해요
As you study more, you get smarter, or
공부를 할수록 똑똑해요
The more you study, the smarter you get

You can use words like “많이” and “더” in these sentences, but you do not have to. For example:

많이 공부를 할수록 더 똑똑해요
As you study more, you get smarter
많이 공부를 할수록 더 똑똑해요
The more you study, the smarter you get

Although “많이” and “더” appear in the sentences just above, the meaning is the same as the earlier sentence without them. That meaning is already implied by ~ㄹ/을수록. Still, Korean speakers commonly include these words.

It is also common to attach ~아/어지다 (Lesson 18) to the final word if it is an adjective. For example:

많이 공부를 할수록 더 똑똑해져요
As you study more, you get smarter

There is no clear difference in meaning with or without ~아/어지다 in these examples. Again, the sense of “more” or “getting…” is already implied by ~ㄹ/을수록, so it is not necessary. That said, sometimes adding ~아/어지다 is inappropriate. Look at the following sentence, which does not have ~아/어지다:

아침 일찍 과일을 살수록 과일이 더 싱싱해요
The earlier you buy fruit in the morning, the fresher the fruit will be

Here is the difference between the following sentences:

공부를 할수록 똑똑해져요
아침 일찍 과일을 살수록 과일이 더 싱싱해요

The first sentence uses ~아/어지다. Its subject is people in general, and people can get smarter. In the second sentence, though, the fruit itself is not getting fresher. “Going early” lets one get fresher fruit, but no fruit actually becomes fresher.

Below is a similar example where ~아/어지다 would be inappropriate:

바나나가 초록색일수록 딱딱해요
The greener bananas are, the harder they are

Here the bananas themselves do not actually get harder. The speaker is only saying that how green a banana is affects how hard it is. You will not see a banana get greener and, because of that, get harder.

The sentence above shows ~ㄹ/을수록 added to 이다. You have also seen it on verbs. It can also go on adjectives. For example:

여자들은 예쁠수록 인기가 많아요

여자들은 예쁠수록 인기가 많아져요
As girls get prettier, they get more popular

You can also make a negative sentence to indicate that as a situation happens less, some other situation is affected. For example:

일을 안 할수록 시간이 많아요

일을 안 할수록 시간이 많아져요
As you work less, you will have more free time
