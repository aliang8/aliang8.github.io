Introduction

This lesson adds complexity to a very common grammatical principle. ~아/어야 하다 was introduced in Lesson 46 as a way to say one must do an action. The previous lesson broke it down, explaining the purpose of ~아/어야 and showing that it can be used without the final 하다. Here you will see more ways to use ~아/어야. It can also be added to a clause that already has the past tense marker ~았/었, so we will look at ~았/었어야 하다.

1

~았/었아/어야 하다: One should have

In Lesson 46, you learned to add ~아/어야 하다 to the end of a clause or sentence to say one must do an action. For example:

선인장인데도 물을 조금 줘야 해요
Even if it is a cactus, you (still) must give it a little bit of water
출산을 하고 한 달 안에 애기가 예방 접종을 맞아야 해요
One month after giving birth, the baby must get vaccinated
이 그림들을 곧 전시할 거라서 액자에 넣어야 해요
I’m going to display these paintings soon, so I need to put them into frames

This lesson focuses on the past tense. You already know how to conjugate the final 하다 in sentences like the three above to say one had to do (or must have done) an action. For example:

물을 줘야 했어요
I had to give it water
액자에 넣어야 했어요
I had to put them in frames
예방 접종을 맞아야 했어요
I must have gotten a vaccination

In those three situations, one must have done the actions. Because they should have been done, they were done.

You can also say that one should have done an action but did not. To do this, add ~았/었~ between the verb/adjective and ~아/어야. For example:

했어야 하다
One should have done, but did not

Imagine you and your partner went to your in-laws’ house for a holiday. Of course, you had to go. You can say:

우리가 시집에 어제 가야 했어요
We had to go to the in-laws place yesterday (and therefore, we did go)

Now imagine you do not get along with your in-laws. You should have gone, but you didn’t. You can say:

우리가 시집에 어제 갔어야 해요
We should have gone to the in-laws place yesterday

Here, ~았/었어야 indicates that the action is already completed (in the past). But the 하다 (해요) at the end indicates that the judgment is being made only now. So you are currently judging that this is something that should have been done but wasn’t. The judgment comes after the action, meaning the action happened without thought of what would have been right.

Below are more examples:

달빛이 예뻐서 사진을 찍었어야 해요
The moon (light) was so pretty, I should have taken a picture
몸살이 나기 전에 병원에 갔어야 해요
You should have gone to the hospital before your body started aching
수돗물 말고 애기한테 생수를 먹였어야 해요
You should have given/fed the baby mineral water instead of tap water
다 먹기 전에 열량 표시를 확인했었어야 해요
I should have checked the calorie indicator before I ate it all
이 작품은 화가가 죽기 전에 출시됐어야 해요
This creation should have been displayed before the artist passed away
집 계약을 사인을 했을 때 중개인과 상담했어야 해요
When I signed the contract, I should have consulted with an intermediary
저녁을 먹으려면 면을 냉동실에서 미리 꺼내 해동했어야 해요
If I wanted to eat dinner, I should have pulled the noodles out of the freezer in advance to let them thaw/defrost

In most cases, especially in speech, it is more natural to also conjugate the final 하다 to the past tense. The judgment still comes after the past action, but now the judgment also happened in the past: after the action, but before the present.

You can analyze the grammar endlessly, but the essence is this: to say someone should have done an action in the past (but didn’t), the most natural form is:

~았/었어야 했다

The following sentences have the same translations as above:

달빛이 예뻐서 사진을 찍었어야 했어요
몸살이 나기 전에 병원에 갔어야 했어요
수돗물 말고 애기한테 생수를 먹였어야 했어요
다 먹기 전에 열량 표시를 확인했었어야 했어요
이 작품은 화가가 죽기 전에 출시됐어야 했어요
집 계약을 사인을 했을 때 중개인과 상담했어야 했어요
저녁을 먹으려면 면을 냉동실에서 미리 꺼내 해동했어야 했어요

In Lesson 27, you learned that adding an extra ~았/었~ to a grammatical principle that already places something in the past lets the speaker say the action didn’t just happen in the past but finished in the past. In Lesson 108, you saw an extra ~었~ at the end of sentences to say something has finished in the past.

Lesson 27 discusses at length the difference in feeling between ~ㄴ/은 and ~았/었던 when describing an upcoming noun. Both describe the noun in the past tense. For example:

내가 입은 바지
내가 입었던 바지

Ask a Korean person the difference and they will say “있었던” is more in the past. Lesson 27 explains that this is not officially true, but it is what Korean people feel, and it is hard to call native speakers’ feeling wrong.

You can also add an extra ~었~ to the constructions in this lesson. For example:

몸살이 나기 전에 병원에 갔어야 했어요

몸살이 나기 전에 병원에 갔었어야 했어요
You should have gone to the hospital before your body started aching

Ask a Korean person the difference between those two sentences. First they will say they are the same. Press them for the smallest nuance and they will probably say the second sounds “more” in the past. Korean people also often say the extra ~았/었 emphasizes that one really should have done the action but didn’t. Both explanations are debatable, but this is how native speakers feel, so go with it.

Let’s look at another way to make these sentences more natural and colloquial.

2

했었어야지

In Lesson 46, you saw that sentences ending in ~아/어야 하다 can instead end in ~아/어야지/죠. For example:

수돗물 대신에 생수를 넣어야 돼
Instead of tap water, you need to put in mineral water
수돗물 대신에 생수를 넣어야지
Instead of tap water, you need to put in mineral water

~지/죠 can also be added to ~아/어야 after it has been added to ~았/었~, as shown earlier. For example:

수돗물 대신에 생수를 넣었어야지

The meaning is similar to simply ending the sentence in 하다 or 했다. For example:

수돗물 대신에 생수를 넣었어야지
Instead of tap water, you should have put in mineral water
수돗물 대신에 생수를 넣었어야 했어
Instead of tap water, you should have put in mineral water

There is a slight difference in nuance. With ~았/었어야지/죠, the speaker is most likely talking directly to the person who “should have” done the action, and giving the advice in a slightly sassy way. The nuance is hard to translate, but it is there. More examples:

위조한 지폐가 아닌지 확인했었어야지
You should have checked to see that it wasn’t a forged bill
덩어리가 생기지 않게 오랫동안 저었어야지
You should have stirred it for a long time so that a clump wouldn’t form
강아지가 음식을 핥기 전에 미리 치웠어야지
You should have cleaned up the food in advance so that the dog/puppy wouldn’t lick it
그 사람이 눈치채지 못하게 접근 했었어야지

고혈압 환자라서 나트륨이 많이 들어간 음식을 줄였어야지
