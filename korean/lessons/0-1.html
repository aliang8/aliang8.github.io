Unit 0: Hangul · Lesson 0.1

Korean Word Structure and Basic Letters

Before words or grammar, learn to read and pronounce Korean letters and syllables. Without reading, the rest of the language is very hard to study.

The Unit 0 lessons give Romanized equivalents for the Korean alphabet. Once you can read Hangul, drop the Romanizations completely. For example, instead of studying like this:

학교 (hak-kyo)
school

You should study like this:

학교
school

Study these characters hard. Memorizing them is the first hurdle, and it has to be cleared. Luckily the Korean alphabet is fairly simple, even if it looks strange to English speakers at first.

The letters in Unit 0 are not taught in alphabetical order. Simpler letters come first, more complex ones later. You do not need the alphabetical order yet; it is good to know eventually, but you have more important things to learn first. For reference, here is the actual order, split into consonants and vowels:

*(This sound is hard to write in English, and it is why Korean speakers often mix up R and L. It sits about halfway between the two. If you say “I hadda good time last night”, the ㄹ sound is close to the “dd” in “hadda.” Not quite an R, not quite an L.)

One important point before you continue. There is no perfect way to represent Korean letters with English (Latin) letters or sounds. The English letters above are the ones commonly used, and they help at first, but Korean sounds are very different from English sounds. English sounds also vary from speaker to speaker because of accents. So no Romanization can be exact.

For example, you will often see:

“K” and “G” used to represent “ㄱ.”
Or “D” and “T” to represent “ㄷ”
Or “R” and “L” to represent “ㄹ”

None of those letters matches its Korean letter perfectly. The only way to learn a sound is to hear native speakers use it, so listen to as much Korean as you can and treat the Latin letters as a rough guide.

So use the English equivalents as a crutch for now, but do not assume the sounds are identical.
