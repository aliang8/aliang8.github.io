Introduction

In this lesson you will learn ~조차, a particle attached to nouns that works much like ~도. We will look at how ~조차 differs from ~도 and how it adds emphasis.

1

~조차: Even one particular noun

In Lesson 4 you saw that attaching ~도 to a noun means something is true for that noun in addition to others. For example:

우리는 돈도 안 가져왔어요
We didn’t bring the money, either

Here ~도 shows that other things were not brought either. The sentence alone does not say what they were, only that there were others. Maybe you also forgot your credit cards, your phone, or your BTS album.

~조차 is a particle with a similar use to ~도 and a similar, but not identical, meaning. Compare this sentence to the one above:

우리는 밥을 사 먹을 돈조차 없었어요
We didn’t even have money to buy food

Like ~도, ~조차 here shows that other things could not be bought in addition to rice. But attaching ~조차 to 밥 stresses that even this very basic, essential thing could not be bought, highlighting how severe the situation is. 조차 emphasizes the severity or extent of a situation. It usually attaches to something very basic, obvious, or minimal and makes the situation sound dire or extreme.

~조차 attaches to nouns, including things that have been turned into nouns. A common partner is ~기 (Lesson 29), which turns a verb or adjective into a noun. Here is a common example:

저는 서 있기조차 못했어요
I couldn’t even stand

Here ~조차 attaches to the act of "standing" to show that the person was in such a dire state that even this, normally a trivial task, could not be done. ~조차 emphasizes how exhausted, weak, or overwhelmed the speaker must have been.

~조차 can also attach to the subject of a sentence. Let us compare ~도 and ~조차 here as well. Look at this sentence:

아이조차 그 문제를 풀었어요
Even the child solved that problem

Here ~조차 points to an extreme: the problem is so simple that even a child can solve it. Now notice the difference here:

아이도 그 문제를 풀었어요
The child, as well, solved that problem

Here ~도 simply says that at least one other person or group solved the problem too, perhaps adults, teenagers, and kids alike. ~도 lacks the nuance that the problem was "so easy" that even a child solved it. The speaker is just stating that a child, along with others, solved it.
