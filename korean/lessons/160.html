Introduction

In this lesson, you will use a familiar grammatical principle, ~느냐, to create a more complex meaning than you have learned so far, and then add to ~느냐 to make it more complex still.

1

~느냐: Whether or Not

In Lesson 53, you first met ~느냐 as a way to quote a question indirectly, that is, to say that somebody asks a question. For example:

입속에 뭐가 있느냐고 묻는 아이가 있었어요
There was a kid asking what’s inside my mouth
얘가 어디 가느냐고 물어봤어요
They asked where this kid was going

Questions are speculative by nature, and the fundamental purpose of ~느냐 is to show speculation. That gives ~느냐 more uses than just quoting a question. It can speculate “whether” something occurs. For example:

그들이 모두 화해하느냐는 모두가 관심있어하는 주제예요
The topic of whether they will all reconcile is something everyone is interested in

Here ~는 is attached to ~느냐 to show that the whole speculation “whether they reconcile or not” is the subject of the sentence. Putting a particle on ~느냐 is no different from attaching ~고 to it in quoted questions. In the examples later (and in the next few lessons), you will see the usual particles ~를, ~가 or ~에 attached to ~느냐 depending on its role in the sentence.

Very often, two opposite words are used with ~느냐 to show whether one or the other is true. For example:

물기가 아직 남아 있느냐 없느냐는 문제가 아니예요
It’s not a problem whether or not there are still droplets left

Below are many more examples:

In Lesson 53, you saw that ~느냐 cannot be added to adjectives. The same is true here. Instead, ~(으)냐 is added to adjectives. For example:

손끝이 따뜻하냐 차가우냐가 문제예요
The issue is whether the fingertips are warm or cold
이 박자가 맞느냐 틀리느냐가 중요해요
Whether this beat is right or wrong is important
이놈이 정말 악하냐 아니냐를 판단하기 어려워요
It’s hard to judge whether this guy is truly evil or not
입속이 깨끗하냐 더러우냐를 확인해야 해요
We need to check whether the inside of the mouth is clean or dirty
