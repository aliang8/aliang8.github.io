Introduction

In this lesson you will learn how to add ~구나, ~군 or ~군요 to the end of a sentence. This form is used when one realizes a new fact or piece of information. Let’s break it down.

1

Oh! I didn’t realize that…:~구나, ~군 or~군요

Like many principles you have learned recently, ~구나, ~군 or ~군요 has no direct English translation. It is better to understand its usage and the meaning and feeling it creates.

Whether attached to a verb, adjective or 이다, these endings go on the end of a sentence stating something the speaker just realized.

With an adjective or 이다, ~구나, ~군 or ~군요 attach directly to the stem of the adjective (or to 이 in the case of 이다). For example:

과학 선생님이구나

Here is when and why this phrase is used.

~구나, ~군 or ~군요 end a sentence about something the speaker just realized, showing surprise at the new information. The sentence states something the speaker did not know before. In this case, the speaker has just discovered that this person is a Science teacher. For example:

Person 1: 그 사람은 그냥 영어 회화 선생님이 아니야?
That person isn’t an English teacher?
Person 2: 응. 원래 영어 회화 선생님인데 지금 과학선생님이야
No, originally he was an English teacher, but now he is a Science teacher
Person 1: 아 진짜? 과학 선생님이구나
Ah really? I didn’t know/realize he is a science teacher

Another example, this time with an adjective.

Imagine you are going fishing for the first time. At the store you see the price of fishing line and are surprised, because you did not realize it was that expensive. You can say:

낚싯줄이 이렇게 비싸구나
I didn’t know/realize that fishing line is this expensive

More examples are below. As you may have guessed, ~구나 and ~군 are informal and ~군요 is formal. ~구나 is very common in conversation, much more than ~군, but younger people often use ~군 when chatting online or on their phones.

해군에 대령이군요
I didn’t realize that you were a general in the navy
네가 매우 똑똑하구나
I didn’t realize that you are so smart
이 시험이 지필시험이구나
I didn’t realize that this exam is a pen-and-paper exam
지구 표면은 거의 다 물이구나
I didn’t realize that most of the earth’s surface is water
생명보험이 그렇게 중요하구나
I didn’t realize that life insurance was that important
이 돌의 표면이 아주 부드럽구나
I didn’t realize that the surface of this rock was so soft
보도로 맨 끝까지 걸어갈 수 있구나
I didn’t realize that you could walk to the very end on this path
미국에서 비만이 아주 큰 문제이구나
I didn’t realize that obesity was such a big problem in America
햇빛으로 지면이 이렇게 뜨거울 수 있구나
I didn’t realize that the earth’s surface could get so hot from sunlight
이 전철에 가방을 올려놓을 수 있는 선반이 없군
I didn’t realize that there was no shelf to put your bag onto on the subway

You have seen many cases where 있다 and 없다, although adjectives, are treated like verbs when a grammatical principle is added. With ~구나, ~군 or ~군요, treat 있다 and 없다 like adjectives.

Also notice that ~이/가 marks the subject of these sentences. In Lesson 17 you learned that one purpose of ~이/가 is to mark the subject of a sentence the speaker just realizes or is experiencing. Sentences with ~구나, ~군 or ~군요 make ~이/가 more natural.

In Lesson 23 you learned 그렇다 (an adjective) and the many ways it adapts to grammatical principles. ~구나, ~군 or ~군요 are commonly added to 그렇다 when the speaker refers to the previous situation and says “Oh! I didn’t realize that.” For example:

Person 1: 그 친구가 어디 갔어?
Where did that friend go?
Person 2: 여기가 좀 불안해서 집에 갔어
He was a little uncomfortable here, so he went home
Person 3: 그렇구나
Oh… (I didn’t realize that fact)

——————————————–

With a verb, ~는 goes between the verb and ~구나, ~군 or ~군요. For example:

너도 암호를 모르는구나
Oh, I didn’t realize that you didn’t know the password either
아들이 수영을 잘 하는군요
Oh, I didn’t realize that your son is good at swimming
장마에 비가 이렇게 많이 오는구나
Oh, I didn’t realize that it rains this much during the rainy season
강아지가 쓰다듬는 것을 싫어하는구나
Oh, I didn’t realize that the dog doesn’t like to be pet
오늘 우리가 그냥 정상 수업을 하는구나
Oh, I didn’t realize that we are just doing normal classes today

——————————————–

With a verb, adjective or 이다 in the past tense, attach ~구나, ~군 or ~군요 to ~았/었. For example:

벌써 먹었구나
Oh, I didn’t realize that you already ate
어제 생일이었구나
Oh, I didn’t realize that yesterday was your birthday
둥지가 나무에서 떨어졌구나
Oh, I didn’t realize that the nest fell from the tree
수술을 받은 후에 식욕을 잃었구나
Oh, I didn’t realize that you lost your appetite after the surgery
선생님도 그때 아주 당황스러웠군요
Oh, I didn’t realize that you were very embarrassed at that time too
학교 앞 표면을 다 초록색으로 칠했구나
Oh, I didn’t realize that you painted the front surface of the school green

——————————————–

~구나, ~군 or ~군요 are used when one realizes something, usually a fact that has already happened or is happening now. But they can also go on a sentence in the future tense, most often when one realizes that something will be the case. The realization happens now; the event is in the future. Such realizations are usually guesses, so you will most often see ~겠다 rather than ~ㄹ/을 것이다. For example:

돈이 없겠구나
Oh, we will probably not have money
당황스럽겠구나
Oh, you will probably be embarrassed

——————————————–

Sentences with ~구나, ~군 or ~군요 feel as if the speaker is talking to himself or herself. This “self-talk” is not really part of the conversation; the speaker is more or less mumbling “Ah, I didn’t realize _______.”

Then why would we need 군요? Why use polite speech for talking to oneself? This is a personal reading, not something from other grammar books: from much exposure to this form in conversation, books, tests and other writing, sentences before ~구나/~군/~군요 are mostly said to oneself.

Within one sentence, roughly 90% of the feeling is the speaker talking to himself or herself. The other 10% is the speaker showing the listener that he or she is surprised. So while technically “self-speech,” part of the sentence’s job is to show the listener the surprise. That is why you should use honorifics when the situation calls for it (when speaking to someone who deserves high respect).

Anyways, that’s just my observation.
