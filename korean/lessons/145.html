Introduction

You have been learning Korean for a few years. You pick up a Korean novel for some reading practice, and suddenly you see a word ending in ~란. Wait, what is this? It looks familiar. It must be an abbreviation of some kind, but of what?

Many learners feel they know what ~란 does in a sentence just from how it looks, without knowing exactly what it is. If that sounds familiar, this lesson is for you.

1

~(이)란 as an Abbreviation of~라고 하는 or~라고 한

First, two of the ways you learned to quote in earlier lessons.

1) Quoting 이다 and 아니다:

In Lesson 52, you learned how to quote in Korean. For example:

원장님이 병원에 지금 온다고 했어요
The head of the hospital said he is coming to the hospital now

In Lesson 52 you also learned that the quoted part of a sentence can describe an upcoming noun. For example:

제가 원장님이 병원에 지금 온다고 하는 말을 못 들었어요
I didn’t hear (the words) that the head of the hospital said he was coming now

You also learned that this applies when 이다 (or 아니다) is quoted. For example:

그 사람이 의사라고 하는 말을 못 믿어요
I don’t believe the words/rumors/talks that that person is a doctor

The 하다 in the sentence above represents somebody speaking the quoted phrase. That speaking could have happened in the past. To show that the words/rumors/talk were spoken previously, attach ~ㄴ/은 to 하다. For example:

If you are not sure why ~ㄴ/은 describes nouns in the past tense, see Lesson 26.

그 사람이 의사라고 한 말을 못 믿어요
I don’t believe the (previously spoken) words/rumors/talks that person is a doctor

2) Quoting a command:

In Lesson 54, you learned how to quote commands. For example:

아빠가 음량을 조절하라고 했어요
Dad said to adjust the volume

As in the previous section, the quoted part of this sentence can describe a noun. These sentences look strikingly like the ones above because they also contain “~라고 하는.” For example:

아빠가 음량을 조절하라고 하는 소리를 못 들었어요
I didn’t hear (the sound of) dad saying to adjust the volume

Again, the 하다 here represents somebody speaking the quoted phrase, which could have happened in the past. To show that the sound/words were spoken previously, attach ~ㄴ/은 to 하다. For example:

아빠가 음량을 조절하라고 한 소리를 못 들었어요
I didn’t hear (the sound of) that dad had said to adjust the volume

————

That is two types of quoting, each with a present and past tense form, so four types in total.

1) Quoting 이다 (or 아니다) and using it to describe a noun in the present tense
2) Quoting 이다 (or 아니다) and using it to describe a noun in the past tense
3) Quoting a command and using it to describe a noun in the present tense
4) Quoting a command and using it to describe a noun in the past tense

~란 is an abbreviation of ~라고 하는 or ~라고 한 in any of the four situations above. Let’s take each one and see how 란 replaces ~라고 하는 or ~라고 한.

1) Quoting 이다 and using it to describe a noun in the present tense

그 사람이 의사라고 하는 말을 못 믿어요

그 사람이 의사란 말을 못 믿어요
I don’t believe the words/rumors/talks that that person is a doctor

If the noun that 이다 (or 아니다) is attached to ends in a consonant, 이 is placed between the noun and 라/란:

그 사람이 선생님이라고 하는 말을 못 믿어요

그 사람이 선생님이란 말을 못 믿어요
I don’t believe the words/rumors/talks that that person is a teacher

2) Quoting 이다 (or 아니다) and using it to describe a noun in the past tense

그 사람이 의사라고 한 말을 못 믿어요

그 사람이 의사란 말을 못 믿어요
I don’t believe the (previously spoken) words/rumors/talks that person is a doctor

If the noun that 이다 is attached to ends in a consonant, 이 is placed between the noun and 라/란.

그 사람이 선생님이라고 한 말을 못 믿어요

그 사람이 선생님이란 말을 못 믿어요
I don’t believe the (previously spoken) words/rumors/talks that person is a teacher

3) Quoting a command and using it to describe a noun in the present tense

아빠가 음량을 조절하라고 하는 소리를 못 들었어요

아빠가 음량을 조절하란 소리를 못 들었어요
I didn’t hear (the sound of) dad saying to adjust the volume

If the stem of the verb that 라/란 is attached to ends in a consonant, 으 is placed between the stem and 라/란. This differs from the 이다 sentences: 으 goes before 라/란 instead of 이.

아빠가 돈을 금고에 넣으라고 하는 소리를 못 들었어요

아빠가 돈을 금고에 넣으란 소리를 못 들었어요
I didn’t hear (the sound of) dad saying to put the money in the safe

4) When a quoted command describes a noun in the past tense:

아빠가 음량을 조절하라고 한 소리를 못 들었어요

아빠가 음량을 조절하란 소리를 못 들었어요
I didn’t hear (the sound of) that dad had said to adjust the volume

If the stem of the verb that 란 is attached to ends in a consonant, 으 is placed between the stem and 라/란.

아빠가 돈을 금고에 넣으라고 한 소리를 못 들었어요

아빠가 돈을 금고에 넣으란 소리를 못 들었어요
I didn’t hear (the sound of) that dad had said to put the money in the safe

————

To summarize: ~란 is an abbreviation of ~라고 하는 or ~라고 한. The “라” may come from quoting 이다 or from a quoted command. Here are many examples. They are not separated into the “이다” type and the “command” type; working that out yourself is good practice.

그 회사가 내일부터 1주일 동안 휴업이란 게 사실이에요
The word/rumor/speaking that that business is closed tomorrow is true
오늘 비가 올 거라 우산을 가져가란 일기예보를 까먹었어요
It will rain today, and I forgot about the rain forecast telling us to bring an umbrella
그 여자가 임신한 아이가 남자아이란 것은 제 추측이에요
It is my guess that the baby that woman is pregnant with is a boy
저의 가장 친한친구가 쌍둥이란 것을 지금까지 몰랐어요
Until now, I didn’t know that my best friend is (has) a twin
땀띠가 있어서 이 연고를 바르란 아빠의 말을 무시했어요
I have a heat rash, and I didn’t listen (ignored) the words/advice from my dad to put ointment on it
심부름하고 곧장 집으로 오란 엄마의 말을 기억하고 바로 집으로 갔어요
I remembered my mom saying that I (should) do the errands and then come immediately home, so I went right home
2

~(이)란 as a Way to Explain what Something is

~(이)란 is also used to introduce an explanation of what something is. If a company’s website wanted to explain a cool new technology called “BNCT,” the heading of the article might be:

BNCT이란?

Right below that, the company would explain what BNCT is. In these cases, ~(이)란 translates well as “what is…” Here is another example.

Riding the Seoul subway is cheap, around 1,200 – 2,000 won depending on how far you go. Still, some people duck under (or jump over) the entry gates to ride for free. If caught, the fine is 30 times the fare. English has no everyday word for this, but “free-riding” is close. Korean has a term too, though not one everyone knows: 부정승차 (essentially, riding the train illegally). Some stations have posters at the entrance with this title:

부정승차란?

Right below that would be an explanation of what 부정승차 (free-riding) is. Again, this translates as something like “What is free-riding?” Here is another example.

Imagine a school trying to get accredited by an organization called WASC. To introduce the organization to the staff, the principal holds a meeting, and the first slide says only this:

WASC이란?

The following slides explain the organization and how the school could get accredited.

This usage is not limited to headings or title pages. It can also be used in a sentence: the speaker starts with the thing to be explained and finishes the sentence by explaining it. For example:

부정승차란 정해진 요금을 지불하지 않고 대중교통을 이용하는 것을 말한다
“Free-riding” is the term given to not paying the set fare and using public transportation
