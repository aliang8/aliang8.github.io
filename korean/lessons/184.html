Introduction

In this lesson you will learn ~(이)나마, a particle whose meaning is close to ~지만 or ~아/어도.

1

~(이)나마: Accepting an Unsatisfactory Situation

You can attach ~(이)나마 to nouns and adverbs to show that you have accepted a situation even though it is not perfectly satisfactory. Here are some simple examples.

물이나마 마셔요
Drink some water
사과나마 드릴까요?
Can I give you an apple?
천천히나마 할까요?
Shall I do it slowly?

As you can see, ~이나마 goes on nouns and adverbs ending in a consonant, and ~나마 on those ending in a vowel. You can also add ~나마 to adjectives turned into adverbs with ~게 (from Lesson 8). For fun, here it is applied to words using ~답다 and ~롭다 from the previous two lessons (Lessons 182 and Lesson 183):

학생답게나마 행동하려고 노력했어요
I tried to behave like a student should
명예롭게나마 퇴장하길 원했어요
He wanted to leave with a little honor

Like many grammatical principles in Unit 8, ~(이)나마 is quite literary and not used much in everyday conversation. It is fine to use, but you are more likely to meet it in literature.

The translations so far are quite literal and miss the nuance. Like ~(이)라도 (Lesson 111), ~(이)나마 shows that the speaker is not fully satisfied with the situation. But when ~(이)나마 is attached to a noun, the speaker accepts it because "at least" that noun is available. With an adverb, "at least" the situation is happening that way. So the translations above are better as:

물이나마 마셔요
At least drink some water
사과나마 드릴까요?
Can I at least offer you an apple?
천천히나마 할까요?
Shall I at least do it slowly?
학생답게나마 행동하려고 노력했어요
I tried to at least behave like a student should
명예롭게나마 퇴장하길 원했어요
He wanted to leave with at least little honor

In a sense, the speaker concedes an undesirable situation while staying positive about something else. This gives sentences with ~(이)나마 a somewhat humble tone. Many more examples:

옷이 남루했지만 단정하게나마 입고 나왔어요
Though the clothes were shabby, he at least wore them neatly
꽃은 시들었지만 줄기나마 살아 있어서 다행이에요
