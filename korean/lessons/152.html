Introduction

This lesson introduces trends in how mimetic words work. There are so many of them, used in so many ways, that exact rules are hard to give. The goal is to get familiar with a good number of mimetic words and how they are used, so you can apply that the next time you meet one.

1

Mimetic Verbs –의태어 – Adding feeling, emphasis and imagery

In the previous lesson, you learned about Korean onomatopoeias: adverbs that add effect to a sentence by mimicking a sound. Another set of Korean adverbs adds effect in a similar way. This lesson presents a large list of adverbs used to add feeling, emphasis and imagery.

Onomatopoeias mimic a sound. Mimetic words mimic a motion or feeling, to give the listener or reader more imagery. Imagine you want to say that you got out of bed in the morning and left the house. No problem:

아침에 일어나서 밖에 나갔어요
I woke/got up in the morning and went outside

There is an adverb for exactly this situation. 벌떡 is used when one gets or wakes up quickly, as if springing out of bed. Using 벌떡 does not really change the meaning of the sentence, but it adds some feeling, emphasis and imagery:

아침에 벌떡 일어나서 밖에 나갔어요
I sprung out of bed in the morning and went outside

Hearing the word 벌떡, you can picture someone bouncing up from lying down.

One fascinating thing about Korean is that there are specific adverbs, like 벌떡, for practically any type of movement, each adding emphasis, feeling and imagery.

How specific? Very. There is, for example, an adverb for the situation of wrapping something up. For example:

나는 붕대를 손에 감았다
I wrapped a bandage around my hand

You can add emphasis with the adverb 칭칭. For example:

나는 붕대를 손에 칭칭 감았다
I wrapped a bandage around my hand

Hearing the word 칭칭, you can picture something wrapping around and around an object.

These words are called 의태어 in Korean. In English they are called mimetic words, but that term means little to most English speakers, because English does not really have the same phenomenon.

English speakers know onomatopoeias better, covered in the previous lesson. An onomatopoeia’s pronunciation represents the sound of a situation, like buzz or boom. Mimetic words are similar, but they represent the motion or feeling of a situation rather than its sound.

Mimetic words with two syllables where one syllable is repeated

These words are made of one syllable that is repeated. The syllable is usually an unusual one that you do not see often in Korean. Look at the first example below: “엉” is not a common syllable. The repeated syllable sounds a little funny, which is probably why it evokes a feeling or image when spoken.

엉엉
adverb to add feel, emphasis and imagery for somebody crying

Commonly used with 울다

그 영화를 보고 너무 슬퍼서 엉엉 울었어요
I watched that movie and, because it was so sad, I cried my eyes out
졸졸
adverb to add feel, emphasis and imagery for water coming out of something slowly

Commonly used with 새다 or 흐르다

비가 많이 오자 천장에서 물이 졸졸 새기 시작했어요

뻘뻘
adverb to add feel, emphasis and imagery for somebody s­weating

Commonly used with 땀이 흘리다

꼭꼭
adverb to add feel, emphasis and imagery to doing something over and over with a lot of might

Commonly used with 씹다, 주무르다

Commonly used with 쓰다

Mimetic words with four syllables, where two syllables are repeated

These words are made of two syllables repeated once to make a four syllable word. This section has three parts:

  • These words can be used as a non-repeated two syllable word or a repeated two syllable word. For example, the first word in this section 펄럭펄럭 can also be used as 펄럭. Repeating the two syllables to create the four syllable word emphasizes the feeling a bit more, or gives the feeling that something was really rocking, shaking, fluttering, moving, rustling, etc.
  • These words can only be used as a four syllable word. The two syllable equivalent does not exist as a word. For example, the first word in this section 모락모락 cannot be used just as 모락.
  • These words are usually about something going back and forth or changing in some way. For example, 오락가락 is a mimetic word that gives emphasis, feeling and imagery to situations where somebody comes and goes.

Adding~거리다

In Lesson 34, you learned about ~거리다 and how it suggests that an action occurs repeatedly. That lesson also mentioned that ~거리다 is used on words that have “feeling.” Now you know what that feeling is: the 의태어 feeling of adding imagery, emphasis and feeling to a situation.

~거리다 can be added to most, but not all, mimetic words to turn them into verbs. Of the 의태어 in this lesson, only the following have no ~거리다 form:

철철
성큼성큼
갈기갈기
절레절레
흠뻑흠뻑

With two-syllable mimetic words, ~거리다 is usually added to the two syllable word. For example:

Sometimes the ~거리다 version, the mimetic verb, is the final verb of the sentence, where it is essentially the action form of the adverb’s feeling. More commonly, though, the ~거리다 version takes ~(으)면서 or ~(으)며, followed by the usual verb. Used this way, it is almost the same as the plain non-~거리다 form.

그 영화가 너무 슬퍼서 보는 내내 엉엉거리면서 울었어요
That movie was really sad so the entire time I was watching it
저는 시냇물이 졸졸거리며 흐르는 소리를 듣는 것을 좋아해요
I like listening to the sound of a stream flowing
주말 내내 뻘뻘거리며 돌아다녔더니 몸살이 났어요
I was busily going around all weekend that now my body is sore
친구들이 제 바지에 뭔가 묻은 것을 보고 낄낄거렸어요
My friends saw something stained on my pants and laughed
음식을 잘 못 먹고 두드러기가 생겨서 밤새 몸을 벅벅거리면서 긁었어요
I ate the wrong thing and some sport showed up so I was scratching my body all night

With four-syllable mimetic words, ~거리다 is usually added to the two syllable (non-repeated) version of the word. For example:

저는 잔디밭에 털썩거리며 앉았어요
I plopped down on the grass
그 남자는 술을 마시고 휘청거리면서 길을 건넜어요
That man drank alcohol and then stumbled across the street
그 직원은 생글거리면서 항상 손님들에게 인사해요
That worker always smile when he greats customers
저는 너무 화가 나서 몸이 부들거렸어요
I was so mad that my body was shaking
술을 바닥에 쏟아서 바닥이 끈적거려요
I spilled alcohol all over the floor and now the floor is sticky
걸레로 바닥을 열심히 닦아서 바닥이 반들거려요
I wiped the floor really hard with a rag and now the floor is all shiny
목이 너무 말라서 물을 꿀꺽거리면서 급하게 마셨어요
I was so thirsty that I urgently gulped down water
많은 학생들이 잠에 취해 꾸벅거리면서 수업을 들었어요
A lot of students dose off in class because they’re so tired
