Introduction

In this lesson you will learn a simple, common way to end a sentence with a bit of attitude: the subtle meaning of ending sentences in ~ㄴ/는다니, ~다니, ~(이)라니, ~자니, ~(느)냐니 and ~(으)라니.

1

Responding in Surprise or Displeasure:~ㄴ/는다니

Imagine a friend comes to your house. He has been there an hour, and you expect him to stay longer because you are both having fun. Suddenly he says he is leaving:

내가 이제 갈게!
I’m leaving now

You are surprised, a little shocked, a little displeased. Why is he leaving so early? He just got here. Do you even believe him? One very quick response expresses all of this:

지금 간다니?
You’re going now? You just got here! Why are you leaving so early?

Conversation Audio

The grammatical principle added to the stem of 가다 here is technically ~ㄴ다니, but a simple way to see it is the plain form conjugation followed by ~니. Adding ~ㄴ다니 to the stem of a verb ending in a vowel implies the speaker’s surprise, displeasure or astonishment at what was just said. Here is another situation.

——

Imagine you are on the phone with a friend at 1:00 in the morning. You assume he is in bed. Suddenly he says he is out at a restaurant eating by himself. You are surprised, a little shocked (probably not displeased this time). Why is he eating alone at 1:00 in the morning? Do you even believe him? There is no background noise on the line. One very quick response expresses all of this:

지금 먹는다니?
You’re eating now? There’s no way! It’s 1:00am. I don’t hear any background noise!

The grammatical principle added to the stem of 먹다 here is technically ~는다니, but again, a simple way to see it is the plain form conjugation followed by ~니.

——

Below are more examples:

지금부터 싫어하는 친구들을 메신저에서 차단할 거야

차단한다니? 그 친구들이랑 연락해야 하면 어떻게 할 건데?
I’m going to block the friends I don’t like on messenger from now on

= Block? What happens if you need to contact them?

Conversation Audio

내일 주식이 다 급상승할 거예요

급상승한다니? 모든 예측은 주식이 내일 다 급락할 거라고 하는데
All of the stocks are going to skyrocket tomorrow

= Skyrocket? All of the forecasts say that the stocks will all plummet tomorrow

Conversation Audio

You can also add this to verbs conjugated in the past tense. Imagine your friend comes over again. You planned to have dinner together, but he arrives and tells you he already ate. You are surprised, a little shocked, a little displeased. Why did he already eat? You were supposed to eat together. One very quick response expresses all of this:

이미 먹었다니!
You already ate! We were supposed to eat together! Why did you already eat?

——

Here is another situation:

이 시험을 한번에 통과하지 못해서 좌절했어

좌절했다니? 그 시험을 한번에 통과하는 사람은 거의 없어
I’m frustrated that I couldn’t pass the test on the first try

= Frustrated? There aren’t many people who pass that test on the first try

Conversation Audio

2

Adding~다니 to Adjectives

You saw that ~ㄴ다니 and ~는다니 are simply ~니 added to the plain form of a verb. The same idea applies to adjectives. Remember that the plain form of an adjective is just its dictionary form, so with adjectives you only attach ~니 to 다. For example:

우리가 같이 있어서 민망해요

민망하다니?
I’m embarrassed because I am with you

= Embarrassed?

Conversation Audio

이번 주에 애기 머리를 다듬고 싶어요

다듬고 싶다니? 애기 머리에 솜털밖에 없다
I want to trim the baby’s hair this weekend

= You want to trim her hair? There is nothing but peach fuzz on her head

Conversation Audio

이 피자가 느끼하지 않고 아주 단백해요

단백하다니? 기름이 엄청 많은데
This pizza isn’t greasy, it’s very clean/light

= Light? There is so much grease

Conversation Audio

걱정하지 마! 물이 아주 넉넉해

넉넉하다니? 너무 없어서 곧 바닷물을 마셔야겠다
Don’t worry, there is more than enough water

= Enough? There is so little I’m going to have to start drinking sea water soon

Conversation Audio

내 남자친구를 부모님이 못마땅해해서 속상해

못마땅하다니? 너 남자친구보다 더 좋은 사람이 어디 있어

너무 짜! 조금 덜 조렸어야지

짜다니? 소금을 아예 안 넣었어

흐릿하다니? 아주 맑은데

Conversation Audio

3

Adding~라니 to Nouns

In Lesson 52, you learned how to quote sentences. You may notice that the rules for quoting a verb or adjective are very similar to the rules for this lesson’s grammar. A table shows the similarities.

The table has four types of words: a verb with a stem ending in a vowel, a verb with a stem ending in a consonant, an adjective with a stem ending in a vowel, and an adjective with a stem ending in a consonant.

가다 먹다 행복하다 작다
Quoting 간다고 먹는다고 행복하다고 작다고
Responding with surprise 간다니 먹는다니 행복하다니 작다니

In Lesson 52, you also learned how to quote 이다. As you might expect, adding our new grammar to 이다 follows the same rules as quoting with 이다; the only difference is 니 instead of 고. Below you can see how this works when 이다 is attached to a noun ending in a vowel and to a noun ending in a consonant.

머리 밥
Quoting 머리라고 밥이라고
Responding with surprise 머리라니 밥이라니

The table above shows how ~(이)라니 is added to nouns, but to be sure:

If the noun that 이다 is attached to ends in a consonant, “이” is placed between ~라니 and the noun.

선생님이 기껏해야 30살이실 것 같아

30살이라니? 지금 45살이야!
The teacher seems like he is barely 30 years old

= 30? He is 45 years old!

Conversation Audio

If the noun that 이다 is attached to ends in a vowel, “이” is not used and ~라니 is attached directly to the noun.

그 사람은 나의 여자 친구야

여자 친구라니? 어제 만났잖아
That person is my girlfriend

= Girlfriend? You met her yesterday

Conversation Audio

In the two exchanges above, 이다 was actually part of what the speaker meant.

What do you mean I am thirty years old?
What do you mean she is your girlfriend?

Often you just want to express surprise or displeasure at a noun and do not really need “이다.” But you cannot add ~라니 directly to a noun. In these cases “이다” is used, but the meaning of 이다 is not really there. The speaker is simply reacting to a noun that was just said. For example:

돈을 다용도실에 놔뒀어요

다용도실이라니?! 거기 놔두면 아무나 가져갈 수 있잖아
I put the money in the utility room

= Utility room?! If you put it there, anybody could just take it

Conversation Audio

Notice that the response above is not translated as “What do you mean it is a utility room!” Person 1 never said it is a utility room; Person 1 said to put it in the utility room. “이다” is there only because ~라니 cannot attach directly to the noun. In these situations, translate the response as the word itself with an exclamation mark and a question mark. For example:

돈을 다용도실에 놔뒀어요
I put the money in the utility room
다용도실이라니?!
Utility room!?

Below are many more examples:

다음 주에 소개팅할 남자가 대머리야

대머리라니?
The man you are going on a blind date with tomorrow is bald

= Bald?

Conversation Audio

방이 너무 더워서 선풍기를 미풍으로 틀었어

미풍이라니? 바람이 너무 센데!
The room was too hot, so I turned the fan onto light

= Light? It’s blowing really hard!

Conversation Audio

이 책자에서 그 영화의 줄거리 요약을 찾을 수 있어

요약이라니? 이 책자에서 그 영화에 대한 내용이 백 페이지인데!
You can find that movie’s plot summary in this booklet

= Summary? The content in this booklet about that movie is 100 pages!

Conversation Audio

지금 금방 갔다 올게! 기껏해야 10분 걸릴 거야

10분이라니? 거기 가는 데만 15분 걸려
I’ll be back soon! It’ll barely take 10 minutes

= 10 minutes? It takes 15 minutes just to get there

Conversation Audio

Adding this Grammatical Principle to Other Endings

In Lesson 52, you learned how to quote verbs, adjectives and 이다. In Lesson 53, you learned how to quote suggestions with ~자고. Replacing 고 with 니 expresses your surprise or displeasure at a suggestion. Compare this pattern with the quoting rules from Lesson 53.

가다 먹다
Quoting suggestions 가자고 먹자고
Responding with surprise to a suggestion 가자니 먹자니

Here are some examples of ~자니 in use:

지금 나가자니? 날씨가 너무 추워
Let’s go outside, you say? The weather is so cold
커피를 더 마시자니? 돈이 넉넉하지 않잖아
Let’s drink coffee, you say? We don’t have enough money
그 회사에 소송하자니? 변호사가 얼마나 비싼지 알아?
Let’s sue that company, you say? Do you know how expensive a lawyer is?

——

In Lesson 53, you learned how to quote questions with ~(느)냐고. Replacing 고 with 니 expresses your surprise or displeasure at a question. Compare this pattern with the quoting rules from Lesson 53.

가다 먹다 행복하다 작다
Quoting questions 가느냐고 먹느냐고 행복하냐고 작으냐고
Responding with surprise to a question 가느냐니 먹느냐니 행복하냐니 작으냐니

Here are some examples of ~(느)냐니 in use:

그 사람이 누구냐니? 우리가 그 남자를 어제 만났잖아
Did you ask who that person is? We met that man yesterday
이 물을 마셔도 되느냐니? 바닷물이야
Did you ask if we could drink this water? It’s seawater
내가 포기했냐니? 새벽부터 이 문제를 고치려고 일하고 있었어
Did you ask if I gave up? I was working on fixing this problem since sunrise

In Lesson 54, you learned how to quote commands with ~(으)라고. Replacing 고 with 니 expresses your surprise or displeasure at a command. Compare this pattern with the quoting rules from Lesson 54.

가다 먹다
Quoting questions 가라고 먹으라고
Responding with surprise to a command 가라니 먹으라니

Here are some examples of ~(으)라니 in use:

모자를 벗으라니? 내가 대머리인지 알지?
Did you tell me to take off this hat? Do you know that I’m bald?
이 책을 요약하라니? 이 책은 너무 길어!
Did you tell me to summarize this book? This book is too long!
