Introduction

The previous two lessons covered several uses of “~ㄹ/을까(요).” This lesson adds one more: ~ㄹ/을까 봐 at the end of a sentence to show that the speaker is worried about something.

1

I am worried about/that…:~ㄹ/을까 봐

First, the word “걱정하다.” It is an odd verb, because there seems to be no meaningful difference between ‘걱정하다’ and ‘걱정되다.’ Since ‘걱정하다’ ends in ‘하다,’ you would expect the active meaning “to worry” (as in, I am worrying my brother). Since ‘걱정되다’ ends in ‘되다,’ you would expect “to be worried” (as in, I am worried about my brother). Oddly, both the 하다 and 되다 forms seem to have the passive meaning “to be worried,” not “to (make) worry.”

Although ‘걱정되다’ and ‘걱정하다’ appear to mean essentially the same thing, when talking about yourself or someone else being worried, “걱정되다” is the safer choice.

You will most often see 걱정하다 when telling somebody not to worry.

For example:

걱정하지 마!
Don’t worry!

Using 되다 in this case is awkward:

걱정되지 마!

Now to the grammatical form ~ㄹ/을까 봐. At the end of a sentence, it shows that the speaker is worried that the clause before ~ㄹ/을까 봐 will happen.

In this usage, 걱정되다 usually follows ~ㄹ/을까 봐. For example:

시험을 잘 못 볼까 봐 걱정돼요
I’m worried that I won’t do well on the exam
비가 올까 봐 걱정돼요
I’m worried that it will rain
그녀가 나를 좋아하지 않을까 봐 걱정돼
I’m worried that she won’t like me
논문을 여름까지 끝낼 수 없을까 봐 걱정돼요
I’m worried that I won’t finish my thesis by summer

To say you are worried about something in the past, conjugate the verb or adjective before “~ㄹ/을까 봐.” For example:

시험을 잘 못 봤을까 봐 걱정돼요
I am worried that I didn’t do well on the exam
비가 왔을까 봐 걱정돼요
I am worried that it rained

You can also conjugate 걱정되다 into the past tense to say you were worried:

시험을 잘 못 봤을까 봐 걱정되었어요
I was worried that I didn’t do well on the exam
비가 왔을까 봐 걱정되었어요
I was worried that it rained
그녀가 나를 좋아하지 않을까 봐 걱정되었어
I was worried that she didn’t like me

Often 걱정되다 is left out, and the sentence simply ends with ~ㄹ/을까 봐. When 걱정되다 is omitted, its meaning is still implied. For example:

시험을 잘 못 볼까 봐
I’m worried that I won’t do well on the exam
비가 올까 봐
I’m worried that it will rain
그녀가 나를 좋아하지 않을까 봐
I’m worried that she won’t like me
논문을 여름까지 끝낼 수 없을까 봐
I’m worried that I won’t finish my thesis by summer

In the previous lesson, ~ㄹ/을까 봐 at the end of a sentence showed intention. The two meanings are easy to confuse because the grammar is identical. For example:

그 영화를 볼까 봐
I intend to watch that movie
시험을 잘 못 볼까 봐
I’m worried that I won’t do well on the exam

As with much else in Korean, context clarifies the meaning. Consider what would happen if you heard the sentences above and mixed up their meanings

그 영화를 볼까 봐
I am worried that I will watch that movie
시험을 잘 못 볼까 봐
I am thinking about doing bad on the exam

With their meanings swapped, the sentences above become illogical. You could invent a context for them, but it is hard to imagine wanting to say “I am thinking about doing bad on the exam.” Paying attention to the context and the content of the sentence usually makes the intended meaning clear.

Below are many more examples:

예산이 없을까 봐 걱정돼요
I’m worried that there isn’t enough budget
다과가 부족할까 봐 걱정돼요
I’m worried that there won’t be enough refreshments
작업을 완료할 수 없을까 봐 걱정돼요
I’m worried that we won’t be able to finish the job
그 소리가 학생들을 방해할까 봐 걱정돼요
I’m worried that the sound will distract the students
경찰관이 저에게 총을 쏠까 봐 걱정됐어요
I was worried that the police officer would shoot me
집주인이 부서진 거울을 찾아낼까 봐 걱정돼요
I’m worried that the landlord will find the shattered mirror

In each example above, 걱정되다 can be removed with no real change in meaning. Constructions without it are more likely mid-conversation. If somebody asks “Why are you worried?”, you can drop 걱정되다 because it is assumed. When you walk into a room and start a conversation, you are more likely to hear “걱정되다” included.

예산이 없을까 봐
다과가 부족할까 봐
작업을 완료할 수 없을까 봐
그 소리가 학생들을 방해할까 봐
경찰관이 저에게 총을 쏠까 봐
집주인이 부서진 거울을 찾아낼까 봐

~ㄹ/을까 봐 can also connect two clauses. The next section shows how.

2

I am worried that/about, so…:~ㄹ/을까 봐

In the previous section, ~ㄹ/을까 봐 came at the end of a sentence, either followed by 걱정되다 or standing alone. By placing another clause after ~ㄹ/을까 봐, you create the meaning “I am worried about/that, so….” For example:

비가 올까 봐 우산을 가져왔어요
I was worried that it would rain, so I brought an umbrella
비가 올까 봐 우산을 가져올 거예요
I am worried that it will rain, so I will bring an umbrella

Notice that the tense of the sentence comes from the final verb, not the verb that ~ㄹ/을까 봐 is attached to. More examples:

저의 여자 친구가 슬플까 봐 그녀를 위해 꽃을 샀어요
I am worried that my girlfriend will be sad so I bought her flowers
그것이 너무 비쌀까 봐 돈을 많이 가져왔어요
I’m worried that it will be too expensive, so I brought more money
집주인이 물이 흘러나올까 봐 지붕을 수리했어요
The landlord was worried that water would flow out, so he fixed up (repaired) the roof
손이 더러울까 봐 소독했어요
I was worried that my hands were dirty, so I disinfected them
그 사람이 내가 무례하다고 생각할까 봐 그에게 사과했어요
I was worried that that person would think I am rude, so I apologized to him
파도가 너무 높을까 봐 바다에 안 들어갔어요
I was worried that the waves would be too big, so I didn’t go into the water
누가 저를 찾아낼까 봐 저는 방 한구석에 몸을 숨겼어요
I was worried that somebody would find me, so I hid in the/a corner of the room
애기를 만지기 전에 손이 더러울까 봐 손을 소독약으로 씻었어요
Before touching the baby, I was worried that my hands were dirty, so I washed them with disinfectant
나이가 많을 때 돈이 없을까 봐 오래전부터 돈을 모으기 시작했어요
I’m worried that I won’t have a lot of money when I’m older, so I started saving money from a long time ago
결혼식에서 이 셔츠가 너무 이상해 보일까 봐 화려한 것을 입었어요
I was worried that this shirt would look too weird at the wedding, so I put on a more colorful/fancy one
주방 기구가 주방에 들어갈 수 없을까 봐 가로와 세로를 측정했어요
I was worried that the kitchen appliance couldn’t go (fit) into the kitchen, so I measured (its) width and length
제가 수업 시간 동안 가르칠 내용이 틀릴까 봐 역사가에게 맞는지 안 맞는지 물어봤어요
I was worried that the contents that I will teach during class would be wrong, so I asked a historian if it is correct or not

You should now be comfortable with the various usages of ~ㄹ/을까(요). Review the three lessons if anything is still unclear.
