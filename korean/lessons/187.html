Introduction

In this lesson you will learn another way to say that one action happened after another. It looks like what you saw in the previous lesson, but it carries a distinct meaning.

1

~고서야: Only After

In the previous lesson you saw ~고서 attached to verbs to show a sequential or causal relationship. For example:

우리 부장은 엉터리 정보를 믿고서 큰 손해를 입었어요
Our boss believed false information and ended up suffering a big loss
전일제로 일해 보고서 그 고됨을 알게 됐어요
I worked full-time and then realized how exhausting it is

You can also add ~야 to ~고서, and the combination means something slightly different. The ~고서 in ~고서야 still means the second clause happens after the first. The ~야 adds a nuance.

With ~야, the speaker says the first clause must happen before the second. More precisely, the second clause can only happen once the first has happened and finished. A common translation is "only after." This is the same job ~야 does in ~아/어야 하다 (Lesson 46): in both ~고서야 and ~아/어야 하다, ~야 stresses that the first clause is a condition or prerequisite. Here is a simple example:

선생님이 지휘하고서야 합창단이 제대로 움직이기 시작했어요
Only after the teacher conducted did the choir start moving properly

Here the second clause cannot happen unless the first is taken as given. ~고서야 stresses that the first action is necessary. ~고서야 is quite literary and may sound formal or dramatic in casual speech.

It is useful to compare a sentence using ~고서 with the same sentence using ~고서야. Sometimes a sentence makes sense with ~고서 but sounds unnatural with ~고서야, and vice versa. For example, here is a sentence that works with ~고서 but would usually be odd with ~고서야.

문을 잠그고서 나왔어요
I locked the door and then left

Of course you can lock the door and then leave; that is a normal sequence. But changing ~고서 to ~고서야 would sound logically odd, since you do not need to lock the door in order to leave. It could work in the right situation, but normally you want ~고서 here, not ~고서야.

직접 해 보고서야 얼마나 힘든지 알아요
You only know how hard it is after doing it yourself

Here, using just ~고서 would be odd. You could do it yourself and then later learn how hard it is, but the speaker most likely wants to tie the two clauses together more tightly. ~고서야 does that, because the second clause "only happens" if the first does. Many more examples:

직접 경주로를 걸어보고서야 거리가 얼마나 긴지 느껴졌어요
Only after walking on the racetrack did I realize how overwhelming the distance is
