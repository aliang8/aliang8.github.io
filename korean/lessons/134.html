Konglish

Korean grammar is very different from English grammar. English speakers often find it easier to learn languages with a somewhat similar structure, like French, German or even Chinese (the characters and pronunciation are hard, but Chinese grammar is intuitive for an English speaker).

Some languages, most notably Japanese, share a similar structure with Korean. That makes Korean “easier” for Japanese speakers (and vice versa) because the organization is familiar.

Still, English speakers are lucky: many English words have made their way into Korean. Unlike, say, a Russian speaker, you can recognize words from your own language.

Imagine a Russian speaker with no English learning Korean. The Korean word for “oven,” “오븐,” would be just one more word to memorize.

As English speakers, we already know hundreds of Korean words because they are borrowed from English. These “borrowed” words are not usually listed in lesson vocabulary, since there is no end to them. Even when a Korean word exists, saying the English word with Korean pronunciation is often understood. Looking around an ordinary room, you might find these “borrowed” words:

마우스
mouse (computer mouse)
키보드
keyboard
케이블
cable
컴퓨터
computer
스크린
screen
폰
phone
박스
box
데오드란트
deodorant
파일
file
초콜릿
chocolate
시리얼
cereal

That is just from glancing around a room. A list of about 350 of these words is at the end of this lesson.

These “borrowed” words are taken directly from English and used in Korean. Any English speaker knows what “키보드” or “시리얼” means.

But many words, although originally from English, are not immediately recognizable to an English speaker, or their Korean meaning differs from the English one.

Because these words come from English, Korean people often assume English speakers will understand them. Without experience of Korean accents, an English speaker probably would not.

These are usually called “Konglish” words. This lesson explains the meaning, purpose and usage of Konglish words you will come across, in this format:

Korean word = (intended English word) = actual English meaning

The lesson has three sections, each with a list of words:

1. A list of words borrowed from English where the meaning is different than the English usage
2. A list of words that are a combination of English and Korean
3. A list of words in Korean that have been directly Borrowed from English

1

Words borrowed from English where the meaning is different than the English usage

아파트
(apart) = apartment building

Translating 아파트 simply as “apartment building” does not do it justice. Korea has many kinds of apartment buildings. “아파트” usually means taller buildings that are long and flat (rectangular), with a few buildings next to each other and plenty of parking and apartment-owned land around them.

In English, “apartments” are usually rented, but in Korea (depending on the building and landlord) you can also own the unit. Families typically live in them, and they are the kind of home most Korean people want. Very few people in Seoul live in a house (called a 주택) because there is not enough land. Old neighborhoods of houses can be cheap and charming, but many people still dream of moving to an 아파트.

Units in smaller apartment buildings (sometimes called “빌라” or “빌딩”) are usually rented (or owned) by younger people, people who do not need much space, or people who cannot afford an 아파트. These buildings are often right on the road with no extra land or parking.

Here are some examples of “아파트”:

이 동네에는 가족들이 많이 살고 있어서 아파트가 많아요
There are a lot of families living in this neighborhood, so there are a lot of apartments
지금은 그냥 빌라에서 살고 있는데 나중에는 아파트에서 살았으면 좋겠어요
Now I just live in a villa, but later I hope to live in an apartment

—————————————

오피스텔
(office-tel) = regular western style apartment

This is another style of apartment. The difference between 아파트 and 오피스텔 is hard to explain. Office-tels are usually designed more like Western apartments: very tall buildings where you step off the elevator onto a hallway with many units. An 아파트 is usually not like this. Each floor typically has only two units, one on either side of the elevator, with no hallway, just a small square between the staircase, elevator and the two doors. Here is a floor plan of an “아파트”:

아파트 floor plan with four Units:

Imagine you live in Unit 2. You have no real neighbor to the left, because the staircase and “hallway” are there. You have a neighbor to the right, but you will probably never see them because they enter the building from a different entrance. And to the north and south there is no other unit, just windows facing outside.

Office-tels are structured more like a hotel: you step off the elevator onto a line of units. Here is an office-tel floor plan:

Here, imagine you live in Unit 2. You have a neighbor to your left and right, one wall faces the hallway, and only your last wall has windows facing outside.

Not all apartments and office-tels are built like this; it is a generalization, but usually a true one. Technically, the official difference between an 아파트 and 오피스텔 is that office-tels can also be used for business as well as housing.

Here are some examples:

우리 사무실은 오피스텔에 있어요
Our office is in an office-tel
우리가 오피스텔에서 살아서 월세는 한 달에 백 만원이에요
We live in an office-tel, so our rent is 1 million won per month

—————————————

원룸
(one room) = studio apartment
투룸
(two room) = two room apartment
쓰리룸
(three room) = three room apartment

A 원룸 is a rental property with “one room.” These are rarely in 아파트 or 오피스텔 buildings and are usually in smaller “빌딩” or “빌라” buildings. A “원룸” literally has one room (a studio): everything except the bathroom, including the kitchen and sleeping area, is in that one room. It does not mean a one-bedroom apartment with a separate living area.

Confusingly, “투룸” and “쓰리룸” apartments usually include a living area plus two or three bedrooms respectively. A “쓰리룸” might have a living area/kitchen and three separate bedrooms (even if one is closet-sized).

Here are some examples:

원룸에서 사는 사람들이 보통 대학생들이에요
The people who live in studio apartments are usually university students
돈이 별로 없어서 원룸에서 살 수밖에 없어요
I don’t have a lot of money, so I have no choice but to live in a studio apartment

—————————————

서비스
(service) = complimentary things (usually food) when purchasing something

Staff in Korean restaurants or stores often give you complimentary food or items. If you are having drinks with friends, they might bring a couple of free side dishes (like French fries) to nibble on. When they do, they usually say “서비스입니다” or simply “서비스.”

Here are some examples:

우리가 식당에서 밥을 많이 시켜서 서비스를 받았어요
We ordered so much food at the restaurant that we got some free food/stuff
만약 고객님이 차를 이 가격으로 구매하시면 저는 엔진오일을 서비스로 줄 겁니다
If you buy this car at this price, I will give you engine oil for free
제가 그 식당에 자주 가지만 거기서 일하는 사람들이 친절하지도 않고 서비스도 안 줘서 더 이상 가고 싶지 않아요
I go to that restaurant often, but the people working there aren’t nice and they never give me any free things, so I’m not going to go there anymore

—————————————

헬스
(health) = the type of exercise one does at a fitness center (weight training)

Working out at a fitness center (a gym) is “헬스.”

Here are some examples:

헬스를 무리하게 하면 다칠 수도 있어요
If you work out too much, you can hurt yourself
저 사람이 헬스를 많이 해서 아주 건강해 보여요
That person works out a lot so he looks very healthy
여자들이 자기 몸이 커지는 것을 보통 싫어해서 헬스를 하는 것 대신에 걷기나 달리기를 많이 해요
Women usually don’t like it when their body gets big, so they walk or run instead of working out

—————————————

스킨십
(skinship) = public displays of affection

Here are some examples:

내가 스킨십을 별로 안 좋아해서 손을 잡지 말자
I don’t really like public displays of affection, so let’s not hold hands
그 커플이 스킨십을 많이 해서 사람들이 그들을 자꾸 쳐다봐요
That couple often is affectionate in public, so people always stare at them

—————————————

PPT = Powerpoint slideshow

In a way this is a direct translation: “PPT” comes from the Powerpoint file extension .ppt. But you would never say “PPT” in English for this. Korean people pronounce it “피피티” but usually write “PPT.” Here are some examples:

제가 PPT를 안 가져와서 프레젠테이션을 못 하겠어요
I didn’t bring my Powerpoint slideshow, so I won’t be able to do my presentation
파일을 다 만들었으면 PPT를 컴퓨터에 넣어서 지금 발표를 준비하면 됩니다
If you have finished making the file, you can put the Powerpoint presentation on the computer and start the presentation now

—————————————

프린트
(print) = notes handed out at a school

Teachers often make handouts so students can follow along and take notes. In Korean these are “프린트.” Here are some examples:

프린트에 보면 그 설명이 쓰여 있어요
If you look at the handout, that explanation is written
선생님! 지난 주에 주셨던 프린트를 잃어버려서 혹시 한번 더 주시면 안 돼요?
Teacher! I lost the handout you gave last week, so by chance can you give it to me again?

—————————————

햄
(ham) = spam

This is a bit of a joke, but true. Korean people eat spam fairly often (it goes in 김치찌개 or 김치볶음밥) and call it “ham.” It is a surprising addition to a cuisine that is usually fresh and healthy, and if you turn down food with “햄” in it, people may tell you it is meat, so it is good for you.

“햄” can also mean ham in our sense, although Korean people rarely, if ever, have a big roast ham to slice.

Here are some examples:

김치찌개에 햄이 들어가서 저는 먹고 싶지 않아요
There is “ham” in the kimchi jigae, so I don’t want to eat it
햄이 아주 짜서 많이 먹는 것이 건강에 안 좋아요
“Ham” is really salty, so it is not healthy to eat it

—————————————

디스크
(disc) = herniated disc in one’s back

This is close to the English usage, but just saying “disc” gets you nowhere in English. Say “I have a disc” in Korean and everyone knows what you mean. Here are some examples:

저는 디스크가 있어서 딱딱한 바닥에서 자야 돼요
I have a herniated disk, so I need to sleep on a hard floor
그렇게 무거운 것을 계속 들다 보면 언젠가 디스크가 생길 거예요
If you keep carrying heavy things like that, someday you’re going to get a herniated disc

—————————————

컨디션
(condition) = used when telling people that one is sick

Korean people commonly say “컨디션이 안 좋다” to say they are sick. “My condition isn’t good” is possible in English but sounds unnatural. Here are some examples:

우리 아들은 오늘 컨디션이 안 좋아서 학교에 안 갈 거예요
Our son is not feeling very good today, so he won’t go to school
저는 어제 컨디션이 별로 안 좋아서 밖에 나가지 않았어요
I wasn’t feeling very good yesterday, so I didn’t go outside

—————————————

In the shower, you would call your hair products “shampoo” and “conditioner.” An English speaker might not recognize these words:

린스
(rinse) = conditioner
린스를 안 하면 머리가 잘 엉켜요
If you don’t use conditioner, your hair gets tangled easily

—————————————

MT (엠티)
(Membership Training) = going on a retreat with fellow students

At university, students in the same major or club often go on retreats to get to know each other. There may be planned activities, but most center on drinking alcohol.

Here are some examples:

대학교에 있어서 제일 재미있는 것은 MT예요
The most fun part of university is going on retreats with other students
이번 주말에 과학 전공 학생들이 다 엠티를 갈 거예요
All of the science majors will go on a retreat this weekend

—————————————

CC (씨씨)
(Campus Couple) = two people dating at the same university

A couple who meet at university and start dating are a “campus couple.”

Here are some examples:

그 커플은 대학교 때 CC였어요
That couple was a “campus couple” in university
대학생이 되면 저는 CC가 되고 싶어요
When I become a university student, I want to go out with a girl at my university

—————————————

팩
(pack) = mask that people put on their face to improve their skin quality

These are very popular in Korea: thin, film-like sheets shaped like a face that people leave on their face for about ten minutes.

Here are some examples:

피부가 엄청 안 좋아서 오늘 팩을 해야겠네요
My skin is so bad, I’d better use a face pack today
일본 사람들에게 한국에서 만들어진 팩은 인기가 아주 많아요
Face packs made in Korea are very popular among Japanese people

—————————————

실버타운
(silvertown) = retirement complex for seniors

Here are some examples:

실버타운으로 이사하면 친구들을 쉽게 만날 수 있어요
If you move to a retirement complex, you can meet friends easily
우리 할아버지가 실버타운에서 살아서 매일 골프도 치고 테니스도 쳐요
Our grandfather lives in a retirement complex, so every day he plays golf and tennis

—————————————

셀카
(sel(f)-ca(mera)) = taking a selfie (a picture of yourself)

Here are some examples:

가기 전에 우리 셀카 한번 찍자!
Let’s take a selfie before you go
여행을 혼자 할 때 셀카를 찍는 것이 유행이에요
It’s a trend to take selfies when you travel alone

—————————————

미팅
(meeting) = a group date where many people meet and rotate almost like “speed dating”

The “팅” in the word is a little funny. “소개팅” is the word for a (usually one-on-one) blind date. Here are some examples of 미팅 in use:

저는 저의 와이프를 미팅으로 만났어요
I met my wife on a speed date
미팅을 많이 했지만 제 성격에 맞는 여자가 없었어요
I’ve done a lot of speed dating, but I haven’t met a girl who matches with my personality

—————————————

사이다
(cider) = Sprite or 7-Up tasting beverage

In the West, cider often means a fruity alcoholic drink. “Cider” in Korea is a drink that tastes like Sprite or 7-Up.

Here are some examples:

사이다 하나 주세요
One “Cider” please
사이다의 맛은 Sprite맛과 거의 똑같아요
Cider tastes almost exactly like Sprite

—————————————

펜션
(pension) = A cottage like house in the countryside that people rent

In English, a pension is money people receive when they are older, or the top floor of an apartment building or hotel.
Korean people often rent houses in the countryside for holidays or weekends. They go to a pension, relax with family or friends and have a Korean-style barbeque.

Here are some examples:

이번 주말에 저는 친구들이랑 춘천에 있는 펜션에 갈 거예요
I am going to a cottage in Chuncheon this weekend with my friends
네, 안녕하세요. 저는 이번 주에 펜션을 예약하고 싶은데요. 얼마예요?
Yes, hello. I would like to reserve a cottage for this weekend. How much is it?

—————————————

내비게이션
(navigation) = a GPS system that you can use while driving

In English, “navigation” could be used for this too: the screen in a car with a GPS that tells you where to go. Phones can do this now, but in a car it is still usually called “navigation.”

The verb 찍다 is usually used for entering the destination into the system. For example:

거기에 가는 길을 몰라서 내비게이선을 찍고 가야 될 거예요
I don’t know how to get there, so I will have to put it into my GPS

It is also commonly shortened to “내비,” for example:

거기에 가는 길을 몰라서 내비를 찍고 가야 될 거예요
I don’t know the way to get there, so I’ll have to put it into my GPS

—————————————

노트북
(notebook) = laptop

“Notebook” can mean a laptop in English too, but Korean people almost exclusively use “노트북” for one. For example:

제가 여행을 많이 해서 컴퓨터 대신에 노트북을 사는 게 더 좋을 것 같아요
I travel a lot, so instead of a computer, it’ll probably be better if I buy a laptop

—————————————

런닝머신
(running machine) = treadmill

Probably self-explanatory: 런닝머신 means a treadmill. For example:

저는 밖에서 뛰는 것보다 런닝머신이 더 좋아요
I like running on a treadmill more than running outside
저는 집에서 쓸 수 있는 런닝머신을 사고 싶은데 안 쓸까 봐 사지 않았어요
I’m wanting to buy a treadmill I can use at home, but am afraid I won’t use it, so haven’t bought one

—————————————

렌즈
lens = contact lenses

The verb for putting in or wearing contact lenses is usually 끼다. For example:

그 여자가 렌즈를 껴서 눈이 파래요
That girl is wearing contact lenses, so her eyes are blue
저는 안경을 쓰는 것이 불편해서 맨날 렌즈를 끼고 다녀요
Wearing glasses is uncomfortable for me, so I go around every day wearing contacts. To me, glasses are uncomfortable, so I stick in contacts and get around each day

—————————————

파이팅 / 화이팅
(fighting) = good luck!

This is often shouted to encourage someone. It fits situations like these:

– When trying to cheer somebody up
– When trying to rally the spirits of a team
– When a group of people (usually a team of some sort) take a picture

You would not normally use this word in a sentence. It is typically said on its own.

—————————————

풀옵션
(full option) = furnished apartment

Most rental apartments in Korea come unfurnished, but (especially near universities) you often see a “full option” option available.

대학교 근처에 있는 아파트가 보통 풀옵션 방이 많아요
There are usually many furnished apartments (available for rent) near universities
제가 가구가 하나도 없어서 아파트를 구하면 풀옵션으로 구해야 될 것 같아요
I don’t have any furniture, so when I look for an apartment, I guess I’ll have to get a furnished one

—————————————

패딩
(padding) = thick, padded winter jacket

“Padding” can mean any kind of padding in English. In Korean, this word means the big goose-down (or similar) winter jackets.

패딩을 입으면 안 추워요
If you wear a thick, padded winter jacket, you won’t be cold
지난 겨울에는 이 자켓을 입었는데 너무 추워서 이번에는 패딩을 샀어요
Last winter I wore this jacket but it was too cold, so this time I bought a thick, padded winter jacket

—————————————

개그
gag = comedy
개그맨
(gag man) = male comedian
개그우먼
(gag woman) = comedienne

Korea does not really have Western-style stand-up comedians. Instead, men and women make people laugh by performing skits (like Saturday Night Live) and appearing on TV shows that put them in various situations.

Every year a few high-school students say they want to become a “gag man” or “gag woman” when they grow up.

In one TV show featuring a “gag woman,” she called a random person and explained who she was over the phone. She said:

안녕하세요 저는 개그우먼 김슬기라고 합니다
Hello, I am Kim Seulgi, a comedienne

—————————————

코팅
(coating) = laminate

To protect an important document, you might laminate it. In Korean, you put a “coating” on it. For example:

선생님! 써 주신 편지가 너무 소중해서 코팅해 놓을 거예요!
Teacher! The letter that you wrote for me is so precious, so I’m going to laminate it

—————————————

에이드
(ade) = carbonated fruit drinks

Western-style restaurants in Korea very commonly offer a drink called “에이드,” usually a fruit-imitation flavoring with carbonation. Some examples:

자몽 에이드
grapefruit ade
레몬 에이드
lemonade
오렌지 에이드
orange ade

For example:

어떤 맛 에이드를 시키겠습니까?
Which flavor of fruit drink would you like to order?
식사를 두 개 시키면 에이드를 한 잔 서비스로 드려요
If you order two meals, you get a free glass of fruit drink as “service”

—————————————

치킨
chicken = fried chicken

Fried chicken is one of Korea’s favorite “guilty pleasures,” typically enjoyed with beer among friends or late at night with family. You can get it with bones or without, called “순살 치킨.” The three main flavors are:

후라이드
fried (no sauce)
양념
sweet/spicy sauce
간장
soy sauce

“닭” refers to the chicken animal, or chicken meat in regular cooking. “치킨” mostly means fried chicken. For example:

오늘 밤 치킨 먹자!
Let’s eat fried chicken tonight!
제가 제일 좋아하는 치킨 종류는 양념 순살이에요
My favorite type of fried chicken is boneless with sweet/spicy sauce

—————————————

컨닝
(cunning) = cheating on an exam

컨닝 is most common at school, when a student cheats on an exam by looking at someone else’s test paper. For example:

시험을 볼 때 컨닝을 하면 안 돼요
When you take an exam, you shouldn’t cheat
제가 고등학교 때 컨닝을 많이 했어요
I cheated a lot when I was in high school

————————————–

오픈카
(open car) = convertible

It is easy to see how Korean people came up with this word. “Open car!” Why don’t we call it that?

차를 빌릴 때 오픈카를 달라고 하자
When we rent a car, let’s ask them to give us a convertible
캐나다가 아주 추워서 오픈카를 자주 못 봐요
Canada is very cold, so you don’t see convertibles often

—————————————

멀티룸
multi-room = a room with many things for kids to hang out in

In the West, teenagers often play at each other’s houses. In Korea that is rare; they go to shared places like arcades or coffee shops. One popular type of place is a “멀티룸” or “멀티방.”

You rent a “room” about the size of a king-size bed. It is not fully enclosed; partitions or walls that do not reach the ceiling separate it from other rooms. Inside you find a TV with video games, movies and TV shows, books, comic books, board games and other entertainment. For example:

오늘 날씨가 너무 더워서 멀티룸에 가서 놀자
It is so hot today, so let’s go to a multi-room and play

Words that are a combination of English and Korean

헬기
helicopter

A combination of the English “hell(icopter)” and “기,” which refers to a machine. The 한자 character for “기” is 機, and it is often placed after a noun to mark this. Here are other words ending in “기:”

전화기
telephone
선풍기
electric fan
거기에 가려면 헬기로 가야 돼요
If you want to go there, you need to go by helicopter

—————————————

믹서기
blender

Another machine word ending in –기: the English “mixer” plus this suffix, giving a “mixing machine.” For example:

믹서기가 없으면 김치를 만들 수 없어요
If you don’t have a blender, you can’t make kimchi
땅콩버터를 집에서 만들고 싶으면 그냥 땅콩을 믹서기에 넣고 바로 갈면 돼요
If you want to make peanut butter at home, you just need to put peanuts in a blender and blend them

—————————————

드럼세탁기
drum washing machine

The English “drum” plus the Korean “세탁기” (washing machine). This is the washing machine shaped like a bass drum, with the door on the front. A top-loading machine is a “통돌이세탁기.”

드럼세탁기에는 문이 기계 앞쪽에 있고 통돌이세탁기에는 문이 기계 위쪽에 있어요
The door to a drum washing machine is in the front, and the door to a tongdol washing machine is on the top

—————————————

골프장
golf course

The English “golf” plus “장,” which marks a place or location. The 한자 character for “장” is 場, often placed after a noun to mark this. Here are other words ending in “장:”

운동장
exercise place (exercise field)
직장
place of work
주차장
parking lot
한국은 땅이 넓지 않아서 골프장이 많지 않기 때문에 골프를 치는 것이 비싸요
Since Korea doesn’t have a lot of land, there aren’t many golf courses, which makes playing golf expensive

—————————————

트레이닝복
training clothes

The English “training” plus “복,” which marks clothing. The 한자 character for “복” is 服, often placed after a noun to mark this. Here are other words ending in “복:”

운동복
exercise clothes
한복
Korean traditional clothes
제가 곧 운동을 많이 할 거라서 우선 트레이닝복을 사야 돼요
Since I’ll soon be doing a lot of exercise, I first have to buy some training clothes

—————————————

셀카봉
selfie stick

“Selfie” plus “봉,” which refers to a stick. The 한자 character for “봉” is 棒.

우리가 한번 셀카봉으로 사진을 찍는 게 어때요?
How about we take a selfie with the selfie stick?

—————————————

싱크대
sink

The English “sink” plus “대,” which refers to a desk or stand. The 한자 character for “대” is 臺, often placed after a noun to mark this. Here are other words ending in “대:”

작업대
worktable, workbench
독서대
reading desk
급수대
water fountain
설거지를 하고 나서 싱크대를 한번 닦는 것이 중요해요
It is important to wipe the sink once after you are finished washing the dishes

—————————————

노잼
For something not to be funny

“노잼” is new slang. “잼” is short for “재미” (fun), and “노” is the English “no.” So “노잼” means something is not funny. For example:

그 수업은 진짜 노잼이었어!
That class really wasn’t fun!

—————————————

M자 (엠자)
widow’s peak (receding hairline)

A receding hairline is shaped like an “M.” “자” indicates the shape of the “M.” Here are some examples:

저는 어렸을 때부터 머리에 M자가 있었어요
I’ve had a widow’s peak ever since I was younger
요즘에 저의 머리에 M자가 점점 커지고 있어요
These days, my widow’s peak has been gradually getting bigger

—————————————

팀장
(team jang) = team boss, team leader

Korean companies and organizations have a lot of hierarchy; each rung up the ladder is another “boss.” Not every company has all of these positions. One company ordered its positions like this:

The English translations are close to nonsense. What matters is where the positions fall relative to each other, which depends on the company.

대리님
assistant manager
과장님
manager
부장님
boss
이사님
director
팀장님
team leader
대표님
head representative
사장님
owner

“팀장” combines the English “team” with “장,” the boss or leader of something. The 한자 character for “장” is 長, often placed after a noun to mark this. Here are other words ending in “장:”

부장
boss
시장
mayor
교장
principal
선장
captain of a ship

—————————————

왕팬 (wang-fan)
a big fan

왕 means “king.” The 한자 character for 왕 (王) is easy to recognize because it is so simple. “왕팬” is the “king of fans” of a celebrity.

Here are some examples:

저는 유재석의 왕팬이에요
I am the biggest fan of 유재석.

One more note on 王: besides “식스팩,” Korean people often call nice abs “왕자” because the character looks like a six-pack.
