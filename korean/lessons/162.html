Introduction

In this lesson, you will add another layer to sentences with ~느냐. Using the verb 따르다 with ~느냐 creates the meaning of “depending on…” in English.

1

An Initial Look at따르다

따르다 is a slightly odd verb: an irregular irregular. When ~아/어 is added, it does not follow the usual 르 irregular. Instead, ~아 merges with 르 to form 라. For example:

따르다 + ~아/어
따라
따르다 + ~았/었
따랐

따르다 means that one follows somebody or something. For example:

사람들이 우리를 따르고 있어요
The people are following us

따르다 is often combined with 오다, 가다 or 하다. For example:

따라와 봐!
Follow me!
저는 저 차를 따라가고 있어요
I am following that car
사람들은 법을 따라야 돼요
People must follow the law
저의 줄넘기를 훔친 사람을 따라가고 있어요
I’m following the person who stole my skipping rope

After a noun, ~에 따라(서) means “in accordance with…” and can refer to laws, guidelines or rules. For example:

길 안내에 따라서 우리는 저쪽으로 가 야 돼요
According to the directions (in accordance with the directions), we must go that way
미국헌법에 따라 사람들은 총을 가지고 있을 권리가 있어요
According to the American Constitution, Americans have the right to possess guns
사람들은 그 나라의 문화에 따라 행동을 해 야 돼요
People should act according to (in accordance with) the culture of a country
규칙을 제대로 따르느냐는 문제가 이번 사건의 핵심이었어요
Whether the rules were properly followed was the key issue in this case
2

Depending on…:~느냐에 따라서…

Adding ~느냐에 따라서 after a verb creates the meaning “depending on _____.” With this form you can make clauses like these:

우리가 내일 언제 가느냐에 따라서…
Depending on when we go tomorrow
우리가 내일 어디 가느냐에 따라서…
Depending on where we go tomorrow
우리가 내일 어떻게 가느냐에 따라서..
Depending on how we go tomorrow

Notice that those three clauses contain a “question word.” In these cases, translations of ~느냐 따라서 sentences usually take the form of:

Depending on (question word)….

If the clause has no “question word,” the translation uses “if” instead. For example:

우리가 내일 가느냐에 따라서…
Depending on if we go tomorrow

As you saw in Lesson 160, two opposite verbs are often used with ~느냐. This works with 따라서 as well. For example:

우리가 내일 가느냐 안 가느냐에 따라서…
Depending on if we go or not…

You can now use these clauses in sentences wherever appropriate. However, the clause after ~느냐에 따라서 has a typical form: these sentences usually end with 다르다 in some way, most commonly as 달라질 수 있다. For example:

우리가 내일 언제 가느냐에 따라서 달라질 수 있어요
It could be different depending on when we go tomorrow

Another common ending is 달리다. 달다 is a transitive verb (it acts on an object) used when one hangs something, for example a picture frame on a wall. 달리다 is intransitive (it cannot act on an object) and is used when something is hung. 달리다 often appears with ~아/어 있다 in ~느냐에 따라서 sentences to show that the fate of something is “hanging.” For example:

우리가 내일 언제 가느냐에 따라서 달려 있어요
When we go tomorrow is still hanging in the balance

미래 is a common word in these sentences, showing that one’s future could differ depending on a certain situation. For example:

얼마나 저축하느냐에 따라 미래가 달라질 수 있어요
Depending on how much you save up, your future could be different
열심히 공부하느냐에 따라 미래가 달라질 수 있어요
Depending on if you study hard, your future could be different

You can use adverbs like “얼마나” and “열심히” in these sentences, but the typical adverb with ~느냐에 따라서 is “어떻게.” It appears even when you are not trying to say “how.” For example:

어떻게 저축하느냐에 따라 미래가 달라질 수 있어요
Depending on how much you save up, your future could be different
어떻게 공부하느냐에 따라 미래가 달라질 수 있어요
Depending on if you study, your future could be different

Technically, those sentences should translate to “Depending on how…”, but as noted, 어떻게 is often used in these sentences whether or not you actually mean “how.”

Another word you can use in place of 다르다 is 결정되다. For example:

얼마나 저축하느냐에 따라서 미래가 결정돼요
Depending on how much you save up, your future could be decided
열심히 공부하느냐에 따라서 미래가 결정돼요
Depending on if you study hard, your future could be decided
