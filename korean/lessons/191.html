Introduction

In this lesson you will learn another use of 말다, in a pattern that shows an action is done halfheartedly or without focus.

1

~는 둥 마는 둥: Incompletely or Halfheartedly

In many earlier lessons you saw 말다 used in a sentence, often with a grammatical principle attached both to something else and to 말다. For example:

Lesson 167: ~ㄹ/을 까 말까
Lesson 168: ~거나 말거나
Lesson 169: ~ㄹ/을락 말락
Lesson 190: ~(으)나 마나

This lesson's vocabulary introduces 둥. 둥 is not a regular noun but a pseudo-noun, one that can only be used when something describes it. You have seen many pseudo-nouns before:

적 in Lesson 32:

그 사람은 저승사자를 본 적이 있다고 주장했지만 아무도 믿지 않았어요
That bachelor claimed he had seen a grim reaper before, but no one believed him

수 in Lesson 45:

그 하인의 눈빛을 보면, 내심 어떤 결단을 내렸을 수 있다는 생각이 들어요
Looking at the servant’s eyes, I feel like he might have made some kind of decision inwardly

리 in Lesson 133:

이승에서 그렇게 불건전한 삶을 산 그가 저승에서 편히 쉴 리가 없어요
After living such an unwholesome life in this world, there’s no way he could rest peacefully in the afterlife

참 in Lesson 156:

화장하려는 참이었는데 갑자기 저승사자가 나타나 모두의 눈살이 찌푸려졌어요
Just as they were about to cremate the body, the grim reaper suddenly appeared, and everyone frowned

둥 works like 적, 수, 리 and 참 above: it can only be used when described by something. Here 둥 is usually preceded by a verb in its ~는 form and followed by 마는 둥, giving the fixed structure ~는 둥 마는 둥. As with the other pseudo-nouns, 둥 is hard to define on its own; it makes more sense to give the whole construction ~는 둥 마는 둥 a meaning.

~는 둥 마는 둥 expresses the feeling that someone seems to be doing something but also seems not to be. The action is not going well; it is incomplete or halfhearted. If a person is doing it, they are not taking it seriously.

Here is a simple example:

아들은 공부하는 둥 마는 둥
My son was halfheartedly studying

The sentence above is not finished. These sentences usually end by saying what is actually happening instead of the action in the first clause. For example, to finish the sentence above, think of what a person often does when they should be studying:

아들은 공부하는 둥 마는 둥 컴퓨터만 했어요
My son was halfheartedly studying, and was just on his computer
아들은 공부하는 둥 마는 둥 친구랑 얘기했어요
My son was halfheartedly studying and was talking to his friends

Below are many more examples:

후덥지근한 날씨에 저는 청소를 하는 둥 마는 둥 땀만 흘렸어요
On that muggy day, I barely cleaned at all and just sweated the whole time
그 총각은 결혼 준비를 하는 둥 마는 둥 하더니 결국 파혼하고 말았어요
That bachelor prepared for the wedding halfheartedly, and in the end, the engagement was called off
행사를 주최하는 둥 마는 둥 어영부영 넘어가니 참석자들의 불만이 컸어요
Since the event was hosted so sloppily, the participants were quite dissatisfied.
아저씨는 경매장에서 물건을 낙찰하는 둥 마는 둥 하다가 큰 손해를 봤어요
The man placed bids at the auction halfheartedly and ended up taking a big loss
그 사람은 허파에 바람이라도 들었는지 수업 시간에 떠드는 둥 장난치는 둥 정신이 없었어요
It was like he had air in his lungs because during class, he wouldn’t stop talking or goofing off

The next section shows another use of 둥.

2

~-다는/냐는/라는/자는 둥: Going on and on with nonsense

A quoted sentence can also describe 둥. In Lesson 52 you learned how to quote in Korean. There you saw how to add:

~ㄴ/는다고 to quote a verb
~다고 to quote an adjective
~(이)라고 to quote 이다
~(으)라고 to quote a command
~자고 to quote a suggestion
~느냐고 to quote a question

In all of these, ~고 can be dropped and ~는 put in its place. In theory this is a contraction:

~ㄴ/는다고 하는 contracts to ~ㄴ/는다는
~다고 contracts to ~다는
~(이)라고 contracts to ~(이)라는
~(으)라고 contracts to ~(으)라는
~자고 contracts to ~자는
~(으)느냐고 contracts to ~(으)느냐는

You have seen some of this before. In Lesson 145 you quoted a command with ~라는, abbreviated to ~란 in that lesson. In Lesson 161 you saw ~는 attach to ~느냐 to describe an uncertainty.

So instead of a plain verb, a quoted sentence in one of the forms above can now describe 둥. This means the speaker is reporting what someone else says, often in a demeaning, complaining way. Typically one complaint (as a quote) describes 둥, then another version of the complaint describes 둥 again. For example:

애가 배가 아프다는 둥 숙제가 많다는 둥 계속 핑계를 댔어요
The kid kept making excuses, saying things like his stomach hurt or that he had too much homework

Here the speaker is annoyed at the kid. ~-다는/냐는/라는/자는 둥 conveys annoyance at everything the kid is saying: that "his stomach is sore," that "he has too much homework," and so on.

Below are some examples:

선생님이 조용히 하라는 둥 줄을 잘 서라는 둥 사람들을 몰아붙였어요
The teacher kept barking orders, telling people to be quiet, to line up properly, and so on
그 총각은 결혼하자는 둥 여행을 가자는 둥 계획만 세우다가 결국 아무 것도 하지 않았어요
That bachelor kept making plans, suggesting they get married or go on a trip, but in the end, he did nothing
그는 저승에도 이승처럼 규칙이 있느냐는 둥 심판을 받느냐는 둥 이상한 질문만 물어봤어요
He kept asking weird things like whether the afterlife has rules like this world, or whether people get judged there
