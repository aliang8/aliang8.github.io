의의 = meaning, importance

Notes: Imagine if I wanted to ask about the pronunciation of 의의. In Korean, I would have to use the additional 의, to say:

의의의 발음
the pronunciation of 의의

의의 is a word. The third 의 is attached to the word to refer to the following noun. The prunciation of this is one of the most peculiar pronunciation rules in Korean:

– The first syllable begins with ㅇ, and therefore “의” is pronounced as “의.”
– “ㅢ” is also found in the second syllable. In this case, “의” can be pronounced as “이.”
– “의” is attached to the word and can have the pronuciation “에.”

Despite being the same syllable three times in a row, the whole construction can be pronounced as “의이에.”

Common Usages:

Introduction

In this lesson you will learn another way to say that one action happened after another, and see how it relates to grammatical principles from earlier lessons.

~고서: Sequential or Causal Relationship

You have learned many ways to show that two clauses are in a time sequence, or that one causes the other. The most common of each are ~고 (Lesson 17) and ~아/어서 (Lesson 37). For example:

Sequential Temporal Relationship Using ~고 (Lesson 17)

샅샅이 찾고 문을 닫았어요
I searched thoroughly and then closed the door

Causal Relationship Using ~아/어서 (Lesson 37)

장치를 설치해서 작동을 시작했어요
I installed the device and then started it up

You can create either meaning with ~고서. For example:

Sequential temporal relationship with no obvious cause-effect:

문을 열고서 방에 들어갔어요
I opened the door and then entered the room

Causal relationship between the first and second clause:

운동을 열심히 하고서 건강을 되찾았어요
After exercising hard, I regained my health

Besides being less common than its counterparts, ~고서 has a slight nuance of its own: it stresses that the first clause comes before the event in the second. In a sense, it stresses that the first action is completed before the second happens.

Below are many more examples, deliberately not sorted into sequential and causal. With these sentences it is often unclear whether the relationship is causal or simply sequential; you need the context to know for sure. Here are the examples:
