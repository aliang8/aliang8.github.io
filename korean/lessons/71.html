월세 = monthly rent

Notes: There are two systems to “rent” a place in Korea. One is called 월세, where (as in most places of the world) a tenant will pay a landlord a certain amount per month. In Korea, there is also 전세, where a tenant will pay a landlord a massive sum of money (usually 80% of the value of the property) and live in a place “rent free.” When the tenant’s lease is up, he or she will get all of the money back from the landlord. When people hear about this for the first time, they always ask “what’s the catch?” The catch is, somebody else (and not you) has access to a massive sum of money for a long period of time. The landlord can invest the money in whatever way they see fit. I’ve had three 전세 contracts in Korea for a total of six years. It hasn’t steered me wrong yet.

Introduction

This lesson covers two opposite words that are very common in Korean: 포함하다 to include something, and 제외하다 to exclude something.

1

To Include:포함하다

Like most verbs, “포함하다” can go at the end of a sentence. There, 포함하다 says that something “includes” something. For example:

이 뷔페가격은 포도주를 포함해요
This buffet price includes wine

This sentence is understandable, but it usually sounds more natural to use 포함되다 to say that something “is included.” Since 포함되다 is a passive verb, it cannot act on an object, so the noun that is “included” takes ~이/가. For example:

이 뷔페가격은 포도주가 포함돼요
This buffet price includes wine
나의 월세는 가스비가 포함된다
My rent includes the gas (bill)
이 조명 가격은 세금이 포함돼요
The price of these lights includes tax
이 회원권 가격은 일대일 트레이닝이 포함돼요
This membership price includes one-on-one training

It is also natural to attach ~아/어 있다 (from Lesson 14) to these passive sentences. For example:

이 뷔페가격은 포도주가 포함되어 있어요
나의 월세는 가스비가 포함되어 있어
이 조명 가격은 세금이 포함되어 있어요
이 회원권 가격은 일대일 트레이닝이 포함되어 있어요

Although 포함하다 and 포함되다 can end a sentence like this, they are often used mid-sentence (at the end of a clause) to say what is included in a situation. The previous lesson showed how ~아/어(서) connects two clauses. You can attach ~아/어 to 포함하다 to say what is included. For example:

저를 포함해(서)…
including me…
저의 친구를 포함해(서)…
including my friend…
세금을 포함해(서)…
including tax

These constructions then go into sentences where you want to include those nouns in a situation. For example:

저를 포함해 여기에 네 명이 있어요
Including me, there are four people here
세금을 포함해 이 집은 20만 원입니다
Including taxes, this house is 20만원
저의 친구를 포함해 열 명이 같이 여행할 거예요
Including my friend, ten people will go travel together
구급차 비용을 포함해 치료비가 100만 원이었어요
Including the ambulance fee, the treatment (fee) was 100만원
다리에 있는 문신을 포함해서 저는 문신 네 개가 있어요
Including the tattoo on my leg, I have four tattoos

In Lesson 43 you learned ~(으)면. Instead of ~아/어(서), you can attach ~(으)면 to 포함하다. This translates more as “if …,” but the sentence comes out the same. For example:

저를 포함하면 여기에 네 명이 있어요
If you include me, there are four people here
세금을 포함하면 이 집은 20만 원입니다
If you include taxes, this house is 20만원
저의 친구를 포함하면 열 명이 같이 여행할 거예요
If you include my friend, ten people will go travel together
구급차 비용을 포함하면 치료비가 100만 원이었어요
If you include the ambulance fee, the treatment (fee) was 100만원
다리에 있는 문신을 포함하면 저는 문신 네 개가 있어요
If you include the tattoo on my leg, I have four tattoos

Now that you can include something, let’s learn to exclude something.

2

To exclude:제외하다

Like “포함하다” and many other verbs, “제외하다” can go at the end of a sentence. For example:

나쁜 성적 때문에 선생님은 그 학생을 여행에서 제외했어요
The teacher excluded the student from the trip because of his bad grades

It can also be used in the passive voice:

나쁜 성적 때문에 학생은 여행에서 제외되었어요
The student was excluded from the trip because of his bad grades

Like “포함하다,” 제외하다 is often used mid-sentence (at the end of a clause) to say what is (or isn’t) excluded from a situation. Place “제외하고 OR 제외한” after the noun you want to exclude. For example:

저의 어머니를 포함해(서)…
Including my mother
저의 어머니를 제외한…
Excluding/except for my mother
저의 어머니를 제외하고…
Excluding/except for my mother
While ~아/어(서) is used with 포함하다 (making 포함해(서)) to indicate what is included, it is not added to 제외하다 to indicate what is excluded. Doing so would be understandable, but strange.

Note that while adding ~ㄴ, or ~고 to 제외하다 (making 제외한 or 제외하고) is natural, attaching either to 포함하다 sounds slightly unnatural.

These constructions then go in sentences where appropriate, for example:

나비를 제외한 모든 동물이 싫어요

나비를 제외하고 모든 동물이 싫어요
Except for butterflies, I don’t like any/all animals

하체를 제외한 온몸을 운동했어요

하체를 제외하고 온몸을 운동했어요
Except for my lower body (legs), I exercised my whole body

열 명을 제외한 사람들이 다 죽었어요

열 명을 제외하고 사람들이 다 죽었어요
Except for ten people, everybody died

저는 축구를 제외한 모든 스포츠가 좋아요

저는 축구를 제외하고 모든 스포츠가 좋아요
I like all sports except for soccer

Notice the grammar in the sentences above with 제외한 and 제외하고. With “제외하고,” the sentence essentially splits into two clauses; you could read them as two separate sentences. For example:

열 명을 제외하고
Except for 10 people
사람들이 다 죽었어요
Everybody died

With “제외한,” on the other hand, the ~는 것 principle makes it describe the following noun. As with any noun described by a preceding clause with ~는 것, we can take the descriptive clause apart. Here is a plain sentence with the noun “학생” not described by anything special:

학생들은 수업을 듣고 싶지 않아
The students don’t want to come to class

As you learned in Lesson 26, you can describe “학생” with verbs. For example:

내가 가르치는 학생들
Students that I teach

Now this can go into the sentence. For example:

내가 가르치는 학생들은 수업을 듣고 싶지 않아
The students that I teach don’t want to come to class

In the same way, 제외하다 describes a noun. Here is a plain sentence with the noun “사람” not described by anything special:

사람들이 다 죽었어요
Everybody died

With the grammar from Lesson 26, you can describe “사람” with 제외하다. For example:

열 명을 제외한 사람들

This is difficult to translate to English because it isn’t a full sentence. However, just like our example from above (내가 가르치는 학생들
the students that I teach) we have a noun being described by a preceding clause. This is hard for an English speaker to wrap their head around, but try to understand that the underlined section of the sentence below is describing the noun “사람.”
열 명을 제외한 사람들이 다 죽었어요
Except for ten people, everybody died

You don’t really need to worry about this, but it is good practice to connect the grammatical dots.

If the noun described by “제외한” is also described by another verb, “제외하고” is better. For example, if the sentence above said:

열 명을 제외한 그 공장에서 일한 사람들이 다 죽었어요

Notice how far apart the describing word (제외한) and the noun (사람들) are. Because another clause (“그 공장에서 일한”) also describes the noun, two verbs are describing the same noun, which sounds odd in Korean. Both sentences would be understood, but it is more natural to join 제외하다 to the other descriptive clause with ~고 rather than use “제외한.” For example:

열 명을 제외하고 그 공장에서 일한 사람들이 다 죽었어요
Except for ten people, everybody working at that factory died

In essence, the noun is now described by a single (longer) clause. Here the noun is described by the underlined part:

열 명을 제외하고 그 공장에서 일한 사람들이 다 죽었어요
Except for ten people, everybody working at that factory died

To exclude an adverb (usually a place, time, day, month, etc.), 제외하고 is usually more natural than 제외한. For example:

일요일을 제외하고 저는 매일 일해요
Except for Sunday, I work every day
다음 달을 제외하고 저는 올해 내내 한국에서 있을 거예요
Except for next month, I will be in Korea for the entire year

Below are other examples of 제외하다 in use.

포도주를 제외하고 저는 이제부터 술을 안 마실 거예요
Except for wine, I will not drink alcohol starting from now
저는 저의 어머니를 제외하고 모든 여자들을 싫어합니다
Except for my mother, I don’t like all women
구급차를 제외하고 차가 이 길에서 운전하는 것이 불법이에요
Except for ambulances/emergency cars, it is illegal for a car to drive on this road
시간이 너무 일러서 경비아저씨를 제외하고 밖에 아무도 없어요
It is too early, so except for the security guard there is nobody outside
어린이를 제외하고 저는 버스에서 자리를 아무에게도 양보하지 않아요
Except for children, I don’t yield (give) my seat to anybody on the bus
돌고래를 보러 가는 이유를 제외하고 동물원에 가고 싶은 이유가 없어요
Except for (the reason of) going to see the dolphins, I don’t have a reason to go to the zoo

“빼다” (literally “to take out”) is often used instead of 제외하다 in these sentences. For example:

나비를 빼고 모든 동물이 싫어요
하체를 빼고 온몸을 운동했어요
열 명을 빼고 사람들이 다 죽었어요
저는 축구를 빼고 모든 스포츠가 좋아요
일요일을 빼고 저는 매일 일해요
열 명 빼고 그 공장에서 일한 사람들이 다 죽었어요
포도주를 빼고 저는 이제부터 술을 안 마실 거예요
저는 저의 어머니를 빼고 모든 여자들을 싫어합니다
구급차를 빼고 차가 이 길에서 운전하는 것이 불법이에요
시간이 너무 일러서 경비아저씨를 빼고 밖에 아무도 없어요
어린이를 빼고 저는 버스에서 자리를 아무에게도 양보하지 않아요
돌고래를 보러 가는 이유를 빼고 동물원에 가고 싶은 이유가 없어요

빼다 is a common way to order something and ask that something not be included. If you ordered 김밥 in Korea and didn’t want ham in it, you could say:

햄을 빼고 주세요
Please hold the ham

The particle ~을/를 is often dropped in speech in these sentences. For example:

햄 빼고 주세요
Please hold the ham

These requests are often shortened further by dropping “주세요” and just saying what you don’t want with “빼고.” For example:

햄 빼고요!
No ham (please)

Other examples:

설탕 빼고요
Please hold the sugar
커피에 우유 빼고요
Please don’t put milk in my coffee
음료수에 얼음 빼고 주세요
Please don’t put ice in my drink
햄버거 한 개 주세요. 양파 빼고요
One hamburger please. And hold the onions.

As with 포함하다, you can attach ~(으)면 (Lesson 43) to 제외하다. This literally turns the sentence into “if you exclude.” For example:

저를 제외한다면 모든 사람들이 갈 거예요
If you exclude me, everybody is going

That covers 제외하다 and 포함하다. One more word belongs here before the lesson ends.

3

Outside of:외에

The word “외” fits this lesson because of how closely it resembles 제외하다 and 포함하다 in use. If you have been studying Hanja (Chinese characters), 외 is 外. This character means “outside” and appears in many Korean words.

Some noun is mentioned earlier in the sentence (or in a previous sentence). Placing 외에 after that noun means “outside of (that noun)” or “other than (that noun).” For example:

그 선생님 외에…
outside of that teacher (other than that teacher)…
그 여자 외에…
outside of that girl (other than that girl)…

When referring back to something from another sentence, “그” is very commonly used before 외에. For example:

그 외에…
outside of that (other than that)

Is this closer to 제외하다 or 포함하다? Are we excluding something, or including something?

Neither. Nothing is being included or excluded. 외에 lets the speaker talk about everything outside of what was mentioned before, whether the rest of the sentence includes or excludes it. Depending on the situation, “외에” can be translated naturally as “in addition to” or “except for.” For example:

저는 음식 외에 홍삼도 샀어요
I bought food in addition to Ginseng

This would be better translated to:

저는 음식 외에 홍삼도 샀어요
Other than food, I also bought Ginseng

Below are more examples:

그 외에 다른 것이 필요해요?
Outside of that, do you need something else?
조명 외에 또 고쳐야 되는 것이 있나요?
Other than the lighting, is there anything else that needs to be fixed?
그 선생님 외에 이 일을 할 수 있는 사람이 없어요
Outside of that teacher, there is nobody who can do this job
한국 사람들과 얘기하는 것 외에 저는 다른 공부를 하지 않았어요
Outside of speaking with Korean people, I didn’t do any other studying
때리는 것 외에 남편이 다른 이상한 행동을 했어요?
Outside of hitting you, did your husband do anything else strange?
