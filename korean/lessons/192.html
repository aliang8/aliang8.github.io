Introduction

In this lesson you will learn a common contraction that gives Korean a motherly tone: the contraction of ~ㄴ/는다고 하다.

1

~ㄴ/는단다: A Motherly Contraction of~ㄴ/는다고 한다

In Lesson 52 you first saw present-tense quoted sentences made by attaching ~ㄴ/는다고 to a verb, ~다고 to an adjective and ~(이)라고 to a noun. For example:

그 선수는 마지막까지 포기하지 않는다고 해요
They say that athletes don’t give up until the end
중요한 발표를 앞두고 많이 초조하다고 해요
They say she’s very anxious before an important presentation
그의 성공 비결은 투지라고 해요
They say his secret to success is determination

You can also quote in the past tense, for example:

그는 시련 속에서도 투지를 잃지 않았다고 해요
They say he didn’t lose his determination even in the midst of hardship

In the right situation the formality of the sentence can change. For example:

그 선수는 마지막까지 포기하지 않는다고 한다
They say that athletes don’t give up until the end
중요한 발표를 앞두고 많이 초조하다고 한다
They say she’s very anxious before an important presentation
그의 성공 비결은 투지라고 한다
They say his secret to success is determination
그는 시련 속에서도 투지를 잃지 않았다고 해요
They say he didn’t lose his determination even in the midst of hardship

Each of these can be shortened like this:

Verbs: ~ㄴ/는다고 한다 becomes ~ㄴ/는단다
Adjectives: ~다고 한다 becomes ~단다
Nouns: ~(이)라고 한다 becomes ~(이)란다
Past tense: ~았/었다고 한다 becomes ~했단다

For example:

그 선수는 마지막까지 포기하지 않는단다
They say that athletes don’t give up until the end
중요한 발표를 앞두고 많이 초조하단다
They say she’s very anxious before an important presentation
그의 성공 비결은 투지란다
They say his secret to success is determination
그는 시련 속에서도 투지를 잃지 않았단다
They say he didn’t lose his determination even in the midst of hardship.

All of these are technically possible, but the contraction is used mostly by an older generation speaking to a younger one, especially parents (or grandparents) talking to their children (or grandchildren). That gives it a "motherly" tone, so you will often see ~단다 on the kinds of sentences parents say. For example:

저것은 위험하단다
This is dangerous

Because the contraction comes from a quoted form, there is a hint that the speaker is repeating themselves. So the sentence above could be translated more closely as:

저것은 위험하단다
I told you, that is dangerous

= That is dangerous, you know

Those translations do not quite do these sentences justice, though. ~단다 is really about a nurturing, motherly feel. It is technically a quoted sentence, but a translation that shows this feeling works better:

저것은 위험하단다
That is dangerous, my dear

~단다 is gentle, since it is usually spoken to younger people, and carries a sense of affection. Another example:

밥을 먹을 시간이란다
It’s time to eat now, my dear

You will often meet this kind of speech in children's books and stories, especially when a parent talks to a child. In the Korean telling of Hansel and Gretel, when the father finds his children after the candy house and the witch, he says to them:

너희들을 숲에 버리고 온 뒤 한순간도 행복하지 않았단다
After abandoning you in the forest, I haven’t been happy for even a moment, my dear
