Introduction

In this lesson you will learn another grammatical principle that contains 보다: ~아/어 보니까.

.

Now that I have done…/Having done…~어/어 보니(까)

In Lesson 81 you learned to put ~(으)니(까) between two clauses. As you saw there and in the previous lesson, one of its functions is to give the listener information from the previous clause.

Notice that the previous two lessons connected their grammatical principles like this:

~다(가) 보면
~다(가) 보니

In those, 보다 literally means “to see” or “to reflect.”

In this lesson, however, 보다 connects to the preceding verb with ~아/어. This is not the same verb as above. Remember from Lesson 32 that this means “to try/to attempt”.

So the meaning of this grammatical principle is not about “seeing or reflecting” as in the previous two lessons. It is about attempting or trying an action.

Remember from Lesson 81 that ~(으)니(까) means something like “because…” or “now that I have…”.

Using ~(으)니까 and ~아/어 보다 together combines their meanings: now that one has tried the action in the first clause, he or she realizes or can state the second clause.

Let’s look at a simple example:

아이폰을 써 보니까 다른 핸드폰을 사용할 수 없다
Now that I’ve used an iPhone, (I realize that) I can’t use another phone | or | Having tried (using) the iPhone, I can (no longer) use another phone

Here the speaker can state that they can no longer use some other, non-iPhone phone. They can only say this after having tried the iPhone itself.

Below are many more example sentences:

연애를 한번 해 보니까 남자들을 더 잘 이해하게 되었어요
Now that I have tried/attempted having a boyfriend, I became able to understand men better
양심적으로 장사를 해 보니까 자연스럽게 손님이 늘었어요
After having tried running my business conscientiously, customers just started coming in naturally
밤을 새워 보니까 잠을 자는 것이 얼마나 중요한지 깨달았어요
Now that I’ve attempted to stay up all night, I realized how important sleep is
유가의 가격을 강제적으로 낮춰 보니까 시장에 문제가 생겼어요
After having tried forcing the price of oil down, I now realize that it created problems in the market
에너지를 발산하려고 운동을 해 보니까 삶의 원동력이 생겼어요
After having exercised to burn off some energy, I now have a will to live
10킬로 마라톤을 뛰어 보니까 뭐든지 할 수 있다는 자신감이 생겼어요
Now that I have tried/attempted running a 10km race, I have the confidence to do anything
살을 빼려고 무리하게 다이어트를 해 보니까 어김없이 요요가 왔어요
After having tried dieting too much in order to lose weight, I now realize that just causes the yo-yo effect

A note on the 요요가 왔다 at the end of this example. The word 요요 is simply “yo-yo” written in Korean. It refers to the yo-yo effect, where one’s weight goes up and down like a yo-yo. Every Korean speaker would know what this means.

Now that I’ve tried/attempted living in a foreign country, (I know that, I can assert that, I realized that) I am very proud to be Korean

One last thing. This grammatical principle can also be written or said as ~아/어 보니, but it is more common as ~아/어 보니까.
