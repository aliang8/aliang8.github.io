1

Present Progressive (I am __ing):~고 있다

Attaching ~고 있다 to a verb stem gives the meaning “I am ____ing,” that is, I am currently doing something. This is called the “present progressive” because the action is happening in the present and is still “progressing.”

This form is very common in Korean. Simply attach ~고 있다 to a verb stem:

As you know, 있다 has many uses in Korean. Sometimes 있다 is a verb and sometimes an adjective. In ~고 있다 as described here, 있다 is a verb. For now this only matters when conjugating in the plain form; later it will matter in other places too.

나는 음악을 듣고 있어
I am listening to music
저의 아버지는 자고 있어요
My father is sleeping
여자들은 지금 영화를 보고 있어요
The girls are watching a movie now
저는 사과를 자르고 있어요
I am cutting the apple
저는 청구서를 지금 내고 있어요
I am paying the bill now

The same form works in the past tense to mean “I was ___ing.”

저는 밥을 먹고 있었어요
I was eating
학생들은 수업 시간 동안 자고 있었어요
The students were sleeping during class
You learned previously that 동안 has the meaning of “for” when placed after an indication of time (10년 동안
for 10 years). However, when placed after a noun, it has the meaning of “during” (방학 동안 = during vacation/수업 시간 동안 = during class)

However, ~고 있다 in the past tense is slightly awkward in Korean. It can be used, but the regular past tense is often more natural. For example:

저는 밥을 먹고 있었어요
I was eating
학생들은 자고 있었어요
The students were sleeping

In most situations the sentences above could be said like this with no real difference in meaning:

저는 밥을 먹었어요
I ate
학생들은 잤어요
The students slept

Still, if you really want to stress that you were doing something, you can use this form. More examples:

저는 백화점에서 쇼핑했어요
I shopped at the department store
대학생은 책상에서 공부를 했어요
The university student studied at his desk
저는 백화점에서 쇼핑하고 있었어요
I was shopping at the department store
대학생은 책상에서 공부를 하고 있었어요
The university student was studying at his desk

~고 있다 can also be conjugated into the future tense. For example:

나는 다음 주에 시험 공부를 하고 있을 거야
Next week, I will be studying for an exam

But as with the past tense, a regular future tense conjugation expresses the same meaning. So although the sentence above is correct, you could also say:

저는 다음 주에 시험 공부를 할 거예요
Next week, I will study for an exam

~고 있다 works easily with most verbs, but there are some special cases to cover before moving on.

2

1) 살고 있다

In English we say “I live in Canada.” You could say “I am living in Canada,” but “I live in Canada” sounds more natural. In Korean, however, “살고 있다” is often used to say “I live in ________.” Both of these are acceptable:

저는 한국에서 살고 있어요
I live in Korea
저는 한국에서 살아요
I live in Korea

살다 (to live) is a little unusual because you can use either ‘에서’ or ‘에’ to mark the place you live. These are also correct and mean the same as the previous examples:

저는 한국에 살고 있어요
I live in Korea
저는 한국에 살아요
I live in Korea

Both sound natural to Korean speakers, though ~에서 is probably the more correct one.

3

2) 알고 있다

In English, almost every verb can take the “I am ___ing” form. For example:

I am studying
I am praying
I am listening
I am walking
I am playing

One word we would never say that way, though, is

I am knowing

In Korean, however, “알고 있다” is very common. Translate it as “I know,” not “I am knowing,” since we would never say that in English.

나는 그것을 알고 있어
I know that
나는 그것을 알아
I know that

While we are on “알다,” here are other common ways this word is used.

When someone tells you something and you want to say “okay,” Koreans use “알다.” One common case is responding to a command. You have not learned commands yet (that is Lesson 40), so for now just accept that “집에 빨리 와” means “come home quickly.”

Person 1: 집에 빨리 와!
come home quickly
Person 2: 알았어
Okay

It does not have to be a response to a command, though. It can be any new information you want to say “okay” to. For example:

Person 1: 우리는 내일 6시에 출발 할 거야
We will leave at 6:00 tomorrow
Person 2: 알았어
Okay

In formal situations you can use “알았어요,” but the formal future tense 알겠습니다 is also very common. For example:

Person 1: 이 일을 내일까지 해야 합니다
You have to finish this work by tomorrow

(You have not learned the grammar for this sentence yet, so do not worry about the Korean. Focus on the English sentence; the point here is the Korean response to that question)

Person 2: 네, 알겠습니다
Yes, okay. (I got it)

3) 가지고 있다
You learned “가지다” in an earlier lesson. 가지다 is a verb meaning “to have/possess.” You also know that 있다 can say that you “have” something. For example:

나는 펜이 있다
I have a pen (remember that this usage of 있다 requires that 이/가 be added to the object, because 있다 is actually an adjective in this form).

With 가지다, you might expect to simply say this:

나는 펜을 가져 – I have a pen. But it is not usually said this way. To say that you have something using 가지다, say “가지고 있다” rather than just “가지다.” For example:

나는 펜을 가지고 있어
I have a pen

Just as we do not say “I am knowing” in English, we do not say “I am having.” So “~를/을 가지고 있다” translates as “I have ____.”

For “people” that you “have,” never use “가지고 있다.” Use plain ‘있다’ instead. For example:

나는 여자 친구가 있어
I have a girlfriend

나는 여자 친구를 가지고 있어 technically means “I have a girlfriend,” but it sounds more like “I possess a girlfriend.” Koreans would probably understand you, but laugh at the mistake, much as you would if someone said they possess a girlfriend in English.

4

Using고 있다 with Position Verbs

In the previous lesson you learned that you cannot add ~고 to position verbs. Examples were:

앉다
to sit
서다
to stand

Specifically, I said:

“These position verbs usually act differently than regular verbs (not just in this situation, but in many situations).”

~고 있다 is another case where position verbs behave differently.

First, adding ~고 있다 to these verbs can be done. For example:

저는 앉고 있다
I am sitting – but note that this only means that you are actually in the process of sitting; as in, you are currently bending your knees getting ready to sit.

“저는 앉고 있다” is grammatically correct, but ask yourself when you would ever say it.

What you want to say is “I am (in the state of) sitting,” as in, you are on the floor or on a chair. For that, add ~아/어 있다 to these verbs instead of ~고 있다. For example:

나는 앉아 있어
I’m sitting
나는 학교 옆에 서 있어
I’m standing next to the school
나는 침대에 누워 있어
I’m lying in bed

This is the same idea as adding ~아/어 있다 to “state” words in Lesson 14. For example:

컴퓨터가 켜져 있어요
The computer is (in the state of) on
TV가 꺼져 있어요
The TV is (in the state of) off
문이 잠겨 있어요
The door is (in the state of) locked

One more time, for good measure:

나는 서고 있다
This means that you are currently in the process of standing up – which probably wouldn’t be said… especially since Korean people have a specific word for “getting up” – 일어나다.
나는 서 있다
This means that you are in the state of standing (probably what you want to say in almost every situation)

Another common word used this way is 살다. Earlier in this lesson you saw ~고 있다 with 살다. For example:

저는 한국에서 살고 있어요
I live in Korea

Adding “~아/어 있다” to 살다 gives the meaning “in the state of living” (as in, the opposite of dead). For example:

그 돼지는 살아 있어요
That pig is living (alive)
그 소는 살아 있지 않아요
That cow isn’t living (alive)

Using ~고 있다 with Adjectives
You cannot attach ~고 있다 to adjectives. By their nature, adjectives cannot “progress” the way verbs do. The same is true in English. For example:

저는 행복해요
I am happy – this is okay.

저는 행복하고 있어요 – I am… being happy? That makes no sense.

In English you can say I am getting happy. The next section shows how to do that.

5

Adjective +~아/어지다

Another powerful form in Korean is adding ~아/어지다 to the stem of an adjective. This changes the meaning from “be (adjective)” to “get/become (adjective).” For example:

행복하다
to be happy
행복해지다
to get/become happy
춥다
to be cold
추워지다
to get cold
비싸다
to be expensive
비싸지다
to get expensive
크다
to be big
커지다
to get/become big
따뜻하다
to be warm
따뜻해지다
to get warm

You can technically use these in the present tense, but as with verbs, the present tense is really only used for common truths or to say how often something happens. For example, you would rarely say:

I eat rice
But you might well say:
I eat rice everyday

Same thing here:

날씨가 추워져
The weather gets cold
날씨가 매일 밤에 추워져요
The weather gets cold every night

It is more natural to use these words in the past or future tense:

날씨는 주말에 추워졌어
The weather got cold over the weekend
기름 값은 비싸졌어요
The price of oil got expensive
그 사람은 밥을 먹고 나서 행복해졌어요
That person ate, and then became happy
우리 강아지는 지난 1년 동안 많이 커졌어요
Our puppy got a lot bigger in the last year
저는 군대에 갔고 똑똑해졌어요
I went to the army and got smart
대학교 수업은 내년에 어려워질 거예요
University classes will get difficult next year

When ~아/어지다 is added to an adjective, the whole construction is a verb. In practice this rarely matters, because adjectives and verbs usually conjugate the same way. But some conjugation patterns (from Lessons 5 and 6) differ depending on whether the word is an adjective or a verb. For example, in the “diary form” these should be conjugated as:

날씨가 매일 밤에 추워진다
The weather gets cold every night, instead of:
날씨가 매일 밤에 추워지다
The weather gets cold every night

Keep this in mind when you reach Lessons 26 – 30, which introduce the ~는 것 principle. There you will learn another grammar pattern that is treated differently with verbs and adjectives. It also lets patterns that normally cannot attach to adjectives attach to this new “verb” word.

As mentioned, you cannot attach ~고 있다 to an adjective. But by attaching ~아/어지다, you say that something “becomes” the adjective, and then you can attach ~고 있다 to that. For example:

저의 여자 친구는 예뻐지고 있어요
My girlfriend is getting pretty
집 값은 비싸지고 있어
House prices are getting expensive
피부가 부드러워지고 있어요
My skin is getting soft

In the previous lesson you learned that you cannot add ~고 싶다 to an adjective. By attaching ~아/어지다 to say that something “becomes” the adjective, you can then attach ~고 싶다, just as with ~고 있다. For example:

나는 행복해지고 싶어
I want to become happy (I want to be happy)
나는 예뻐지고 싶어
I want to become pretty (I want to be pretty)

That’s it!
