Introduction

In this lesson you will learn about 마련. It is technically a noun, but it works more like a grammatical principle.

1

~기 마련이다: Bound to Occur

In Lesson 29 you saw that attaching ~기 to a verb or adjective turns it (and the clause before it) into a noun. There are many reasons to do this. For example:

~기 시작하다: to start

연맹 내부에서 의견 충돌이 자주 발생하면서 균열이 생기기 시작했어요
As internal conflicts frequently arose within the federation, cracks began to form

~기 싫다: to not want to do, to not like

벽에 생긴 균열을 볼 때마다 불안해서 그 집에 들어가기 싫어요
Every time I see the crack in the wall, I feel uneasy and don’t want to go into that house

Another common word that follows ~기 is 마련. 마련 is a noun, but it is hard to translate on its own; the whole structure usually gets translated together. 이다 attaches to 마련 and the whole thing works as ~기 마련이다.

~기 마련이다 has a specific use: you say it when a situation is naturally, inevitably going to happen. The thing that is naturally expected goes before ~기 마련이다. One of the clearest examples of something inevitable is that people eventually pass away. So you could say:

사람들은 언젠가 죽기 마련이에요
People will inevitably pass away someday

마련 is commonly translated as "bound to…" For example:

사람들은 죽기 마련이에요
People are bound to pass away someday
모든 사람들은 실수를 하기 마련이에요
Everyone is bound to make mistakes
사람들은 추운 날씨에 감기에 걸리기 마련이에요
People are bound to catch a cold in cold weather
