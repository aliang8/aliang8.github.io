This is another difficult word lesson: the vocabulary is the lesson. As in earlier “difficult word” lessons, these words cannot be understood just by looking up a dictionary translation; they need a detailed description. You will learn the following words:

대로 = to be done the same as another action

Common Usages:

그대로

순서대로
in order
예상대로

마음대로

절차대로

예정대로

Examples:

그 영화는 제가 예상한 대로 재미있었어요

제가 들은 대로 이 지역 호박은 진짜 맛있어요

제가 본 대로 인종차별은 그 나라에서 큰 문제예요

제가 생각했던 대로 악기를 연주하는 것은 어려워요
As I thought, playing an instrument is very difficult
제가 느꼈던 대로 그 사람의 첫인상은 제 예상과 맞아요
As I felt, the (my) first impression of that person met my expectations
– 그대로 = for something to be done as it once was

Common Usages:

그대로 남기다
to leave something as it is
그대로 내버려두다
to leave something as it is
그대도 떠나다
to leave everything behind as it is/was

Examples:

그대로 움직이지 마
Don’t move, and stay the way you are
이 상황을 그대로 그냥 인정하세요
Just accept the situation as it is
아무것도 만지지 말고 그냥 그대로 두세요
Don’t touch anything – just leave it the way it is
음식을 그대로 남긴 채 식당에서 나갔어요
We left the restaurant with food left on the table
이 생태계를 그대로 보전하기 위해서는 모두의 노력이 필요해요
We need the help of everybody to preserve this ecosystem the way it is
– 마음대로 = to do something as one’s heart desires

Examples:

그것을 마음 대로 하세요
Do that as you wish
먹고 싶은 것을 마음대로 고르세요
Choose the food that you want to eat (whatever you want)

저는 유럽에 갈 건데 어디 갈지 정하지 않았어요. 제가 그냥 거기 가서 마음대로

여행할 거예요
I’m going to Europe, but I haven’t set where I will go. I will just go there, and travel wherever I want (wherever my heart desires)
인기 = popularity

Common Usages:

인기가 많다
to have a lot of popularity (to be popular)
인기가 없다
to not have a lot of popularity (to not be popular)
인기를 끌다
for something to get popular
인기를 얻다
for something to gain in popularity
인기가 떨어지다
for one’s popularity to fall

Examples:

그 만화책은 성인들에게 인기가 많아요
That comic book is popular among adults
그 물품이 영화에 나와서 인기를 얻었어요
That product was in a movie, so it got popular
그 선생님은 키가 커서 학생들한테 인기가 많아요
That teacher is popular among students because he is tall
그 식당은 이 지역 주민들에게 인기가 제일 많아요
That restaurant is the most popular among residents of this area
– 끌다 = to pull, to drag

This word follows the ㄹ irregular.

Common Usages:

수레를 끌다
to pull a cart
끌어안다
to pull in and hug

Examples:

그 모자는 색깔이 예뻐서 인기를 끌고 있어요
That hat is getting popular because the color is pretty
종종 할머니, 할아버지들은 수레를 끌고 주변에 종이를 모아요
Occasionally senior citizens pull carts and gather/collect the paper that is around
그 가수가 새로운 노래를 만들어서 이제 인기를 끌고 있어요
That singer made a new song, and now he/she is getting popular
그 선수가 세계에서 하키를 제일 잘해서 인기를 많이 끌고 있어요
That athlete/player is the best hockey player in the world, so he/she is getting popular
당연하다 = to be obvious

Kids these days often replace 당연하다 with “당근” which actually means “carrot” as a joke. For example:

Person 1: 내일 갈 거야?
Are you going tomorrow?
Person 2: 당근!
Of course!

Common Usages:

당연히
obviously
당연하지
of course!

Examples:

저는 내일 당연히 갈 거예요
I am obviously going tomorrow
그가 그 여자를 사랑하는 것은 당연해요
It is obvious that he loves that girl
그 사람이 지난 1 년 동안 아주 열심히 일해서 그가 승진되는 게 당연한 거예요
That person worked very hard during the last year, so it is obvious that he is getting promoted

선생님이 왜 화가 났냐고? 선생님에게 말대답을 했잖아! 선생님에게 그렇게

맞서면 당연히 선생님이 화가 나실 거예요
Did you just ask why the teacher was mad? You should know that (it is because) you talked back to him! If you oppose the teacher like that, obviously he is going to get mad
아, 당연하지. 안녕? 드디어 이렇게 처음으로 말을 하네, 근데 무슨 일이야?
Ah, of course! Hi! Finally we talk (like this) for the first time. But… for what reason are you introducing yourself to me? (What’s up? What’s on your mind?)
알맞다 = to go well with

Examples:

저 책상은 이 방에 알맞아요
That desk looks good in this room
저 사진은 저 액자에 알맞아요
That picture looks good in that frame
운동할 때 알맞은 자세로 해야 돼요
When you exercise, you need to do so with the correct posture
그 여자가 입고 있는 옷은 이 축제에 알맞아요
The clothes that girl is wearing is perfect for this occasion/festival
– 액자 = picture frame

Examples:

저 사진은 저 액자에 알맞아요
That picture looks good in that frame
이 그림들을 곧 전시할 거라서 액자에 넣어야 해요
I’m going to display these paintings soon, so I need to put them into frames
전체 = the whole of something

Examples:

학교 전체가 닫혀 있어요
The whole school is closed
그 행성 전체에 물이 있어요

우리는 도시 전체를 걸어 다녔어요
We walked around the whole city
내일 이 지방 전체에 비가 올 거예요
It will rain in this entire area tomorrow
그 메시지가 학교전체에 방송되었어요
That message was broadcasted throughout the whole school
해질녘에 하늘 전체는 아름다운 색깔로 변했다
At sunset, the entire sky turned a beautiful color
화가가 전체 그림의 4 분의 1 을 벽에 그렸어요
The painter painted one quarter of the whole painting on the wall
어제 한 수술 때문에 저의 몸 전체가 너무 아파요
Because of the surgery I had yesterday, my entire body is sore
부장님이 회사원 몇 명을 해고할 거라고 해서 회사 전체가 긴장했어요
The whole company was nervous because the boss said he will fire some workers
그 소문이 마을 전체에 점점 알려져 가지고 결국 그 소문의 주인공은 마을을 떠났어요
That rumor slowly got more and more known around the whole town, so in the end the person left town

And the construction: ~바로 그 때문이다

Let’s get started.

1

As Experienced:대로

대로 is a noun that must be described by a preceding clause. You know many nouns like this already: 지 (Lesson 30), 적 (Lesson 32), 수 (Lesson 45) and 데 (Lesson 77). A clause describes 대로 to show that an action is done in the same way or form as one “experiences” an event.

Events often happened in the past, and people often refer to those experiences when speaking. So words that refer to experience, like seeing, hearing, feeling, expecting or guessing, often describe 대로 in the past tense. For example:

제가 봤던 대로 / 제가 본 대로
As I saw
제가 들었던 대로 / 제가 들은 대로
As I heard
제가 느꼈던 대로 / 제가 느낀 대로
As I felt
제가 예상했던 대로 / 제가 예상한 대로
As I expected
제가 기대했던 대로 / 제가 기대한 대로
As I expected
제가 짐작했던 대로 / 제가 짐작한 대로
As I guessed
제가 생각했던 대로 / 제가 생각한 대로
As I thought

The difference between ~ㄴ/은 and ~았/었던 is not covered in this lesson. For a discussion, see Lesson 27.

The constructions above can go into sentences, for example:

그 영화는 제가 예상한 대로 재미있었어요
As I expected, that movie was funny
제가 들은 대로 이 지역 호박은 진짜 맛있어요
As I heard, the pumpkins in this area are very delicious
제가 본 대로 인종차별은 그 나라에서 큰 문제예요
As I saw, racism (discrimination based on race) is a big problem in that country
제가 생각했던 대로 악기를 연주하는 것은 어려워요
As I thought, playing an instrument is very difficult
제가 느꼈던 대로 그 사람의 첫인상은 제 예상과 맞아요
As I felt, the (my) first impression of that person met my expectations
제가 기대했던 대로 그 나쁜 학생이 학교에서 자퇴했어요
As I expected, that bad student dropped out of school
제가 짐작했던 대로 우리 회사는 저를 승진시키지 않을 거예요
As I guessed, my company will not promote me

대로 can also be described by a clause in the present tense. The sentence then implies that one is still experiencing the situation. For example:

그 영화는 제가 예상하는 대로 흘러가고 있어요
The movie is progressing (along) as I expect

Two common verbs that describe 대로 in the present tense are 알다 (to know) and 시키다 (to order). For example:

내가 시키는 대로 해 줘
Please do it as I ask (as I order)
문제를 아는 대로 풀어 봐
Solve the problem as you know it

The meaning of 대로 is built into these two common adverbs:

마음대로
to do something as one’s heart desires
그대로
for something to be done as it once was
Remember the purpose of “그” on its own: it refers to a previous or known situation. “그대로” indicates that something is done just as that previous/known situation.

For example:

이 상황을 그대로 그냥 인정하세요
Just accept the situation as it is
아무것도 만지지 말고 그냥 그대로 두세요
Don’t touch anything – just leave it the way it is
먹고 싶은 것을 마음대로 고르세요
Choose the food that you want to eat (whatever you want)
저는 유럽에 갈 건데 어디 갈지 정하지 않았어요. 제가 그냥 거기 가서 마음대로 여행할 거예요
I’m going to Europe, but I haven’t set where I will go. I will just go there, and travel wherever I want (wherever my heart desires)

Popular:인기

인기 is not that hard a word; its meaning (popularity) is usually the same everywhere. But the verbs that act on 인기 are not the ones you would expect. The easiest and most intuitive is 많다, to say somebody or something has a lot of popularity. For example:

그 만화책은 성인들에게 인기가 많아요
That comic book is popular among adults
그 선생님은 키가 커서 학생들한테 인기가 많아요
That teacher is popular (has a lot of popularity) among students because he is tall
그 식당은 이 지역 주민들에게 인기가 제일 많아요
That restaurant is the most popular among residents of this area

The group that something is popular with takes ~한테/에게/께.

A common verb with 인기 is 끌다, “to pull.” Like 많다, 끌다 shows that one is popular (or has a lot of popularity). In the present tense, 끌다 usually takes a present progressive form (~고 있다, ~끄는 중이다). For example:

그 모자는 색깔이 예뻐서 인기를 끌고 있어요

그 모자는 색깔이 예뻐서 인기를 끄는 중이에요
That hat is getting popular because the color is pretty

그 가수가 새로운 노래를 만들어서 이제 인기를 끌고 있어요

그 가수가 새로운 노래를 만들어서 이제 인기를 끄는 중이에요
That singer made a new song, and now he/she is getting popular

그 선수가 세계에서 하키를 제일 잘해서 인기를 많이 끌고 있어요

그 선수가 세계에서 하키를 제일 잘해서 인기를 많이 끄는 중이에요
That athlete/player is the best hockey player in the world, so he/she is getting popular

The particle ~를/을 is used instead of ~이/가 because 끌다 is a verb.

If something grows in popularity, use the verb 얻다 (to gain). For example:

그 물품이 영화에 나와서 인기를 얻었어요
That product was in a movie, so it got popular
그 회사원이 승진을 해서 동료들한테 인기를 얻었어요
That worker was promoted and gained popularity with his coworkers
우리 사업은 자연 재료만 사용하기 시작한 후에 인기를 얻었어요
After our company started only using natural ingredients, we became popular

If something drops in popularity, use the verb 떨어지다 (to drop). For example:

그 노래의 인기는 떨어졌어요
That song’s popularity dropped
아이폰 인기는 이제 떨어지고 있어요
The iPhone’s popularity is dropping now
그 소문으로 인해 대통령 인기가 떨어졌어요
The president’s popularity dropped due to that rumor

You can also use 있다/없다 to say that something is or isn’t popular:

그 영화는 인기가 있어요
That movie is popular
그 영화는 인기가 없어요
That movie isn’t popular
미국 대통령은 인기가 있어요
The American president is popular
미국 대통령은 인기가 없어요
The American president isn’t popular
2

To be Obvious, Correct, Natural:당연하다

당연하다 is hard to translate perfectly. A common definition is “natural” or “correct,” but “obvious” is more accurate.

We start with its adverb form 당연히. Examples of the adverb make the meaning of 당연하다 easier to grasp.

Look at the following examples:

그는 당연히 왔어요
He obviously came… (Of course he came! Why wouldn’t he come?)
저는 내일 당연히 갈 거예요
I am obviously going tomorrow (Of course I am going tomorrow! Why wouldn’t I go?)

Dictionaries usually give “natural” for 당연하다, which could also work. Notice the similarities in these English translations:

그는 당연히 왔어요
Naturally, he came yesterday
저는 내일 당연히 갈 거예요
Naturally, I will go tomorrow

If somebody asks you a question and you want to answer “Of course!” or “Obviously!”, use 당연하다. For example:

Person 1: 내일 갈 거예요?
Are you going tomorrow?
Person 2: 당연해요!
Of course!

You have not yet learned ~지 (informal) or ~죠 (formal) at the end of a word; they come in Lesson 93, so do not worry about ~지/죠 yet. When answering a question with “Of course!” or “Obviously!”, you can attach ~지/죠 to 당연하다. The meaning does not change here; it is just for effect. For example:

Person 1: 내일 갈 거야?
Are you going tomorrow?
Person 2: 당연하지!!
Of course!!

Kids these days often replace 당연하다 with 당근, which actually means “carrot,” as a joke. For example:

Person 1: 내일 갈 거야?
Are you going tomorrow?
Person 2: 당근!
Of course!

It is also common to attach 이다 to 당근, which lets ~지/죠 attach too. For example:

Person 1: 내일 갈 거야?
Are you going tomorrow?
Person 2: 당근이지!
Of course!

Use 당근 like this with a Korean person and you will get a laugh.

당연하다 is also used as an adjective. Knowing it as an adverb, you will have no trouble with 당연하다 as an adjective.

As an adjective, 당연하다 describes situations that are obvious. For example:

당연한 거예요
(That is) an obvious thing
그가 그 여자를 사랑하는 것은 당연해요
It is obvious that he loves that girl
그 사람이 지난 1년 동안 아주 열심히 일해서 그가 승진되는 게 당연한 거예요
That person worked very hard during the last year, so it is obvious that he is getting promoted
그 가수가 다른 여자와 바람을 피운 후에 가수의 인기가 떨어진 게 당연한 거예요
After that singer cheated on his wife (with another girl), it is obvious that his popularity dropped

The four statements above say that something is obvious. In English, we often do this by asking “isn’t it obvious?” For example:

It’s it obvious that he loves that girl?

In Korean, you can describe an “obvious situation” with 당연한 게 아니에요? at the end of a sentence. For example:

그가 그 여자를 사랑하는 것은 당연한 게 아니에요?
Isn’t it obvious that he loves that girl?
그 사람이 지난 1년 동안 아주 열심히 일해서 그가 승진되는 게 당연한 게 아니에요?
That person worked very hard during the last year, so isn’t it obvious that he is getting promoted?
그 가수가 다른 여자와 바람을 피운 후에 가수의 인기가 떨어진 게 당연한 게 아니에요?
After that singer cheated on his wife (with another girl), isn’t it obvious that his popularity dropped?
3

알맞다: Appropriate/Suitable

Learners often meet 알맞다 and 당연하다 at the same time and find them hard to tell apart. With a good grasp of 당연하다, you should have no problem.

알맞다 is used when two or more things go well together, usually in appearance, taste or smell. For example:

저 책상은 이 방에 알맞아요
That desk looks good in this room
저 사진은 저 액자에 알맞아요
That picture looks good in that frame

A similar word is 어울리다, which also works in these situations. For example:

저 책상은 이 방에 어울려요
That desk looks good in this room
저 사진은 저 액자에 어울려요
That picture looks good in that frame

어울리다 is also used to say that two or more people get along. For example:

그 사람은 친구와 잘 어울려요
That person gets along well with his/her friend
저는 우리 장모님과 잘 어울려요
I get along well with my mother-in-law
4

The whole __:전체

전체 is a noun meaning “the whole” of something. It is usually placed after another noun to refer to that entire noun.

If you have kept up with Hanja, the characters show how this meaning arises: 전 (全) means “all” and 체 (體) means a body or thing.

Here are examples of 전체 after a noun to refer to the whole noun:

학교 전체
the whole school
도시 전체
the whole city
행성 전체
the whole planet
회사 전체
the whole company

These can then go into sentences:

학교 전체가 닫혀 있어요
The whole school is closed
그 행성 전체에 물이 있어요
There is water on that whole planet
우리는 도시 전체를 걸어 다녔어요
We walked around the whole city
부장님이 회사원 몇 명을 해고할 거라고 해서 회사 전체가 긴장했어요
The whole company was nervous because the boss said he will fire some workers
5

That is why … :바로 그때문이다

At this stage, learners often want to know how to make sentences like these:

That is why I want to go!
That is why I like her!
That is why I am learning Korean!

In other words, how to say “That’s why…” in Korean. The best answer is:

… 바로 그 때문이다

First, think about when English uses this kind of sentence. You say it when some situation is being discussed and you want to say that it is the reason you do something. You do not state the situation; you just point to it with “that.” Imagine you heard that your best friend is going to a party tonight. You can say “That is the reason I want to go.”

You do not need to say “Because my best friend is going, I want to go.” Since “my friend going” was already discussed, “That is the reason I want to go” is enough.

바로 그 때문이다 means “That is the reason.” 그 refers to the situation already known, and 때문에 refers to the reason. 바로 means something like “precisely,” stressing that that is the reason. For example:

That is precisely the reason…

Next we need to state the action that results from this known situation. We turn the result into a noun with ~는 것. For example:

제가 가고 싶은 것
(the noun of “I want to go”)
제가 그녀를 좋아하는 것
(the noun of “I like her”)
제가 한국어를 배우고 있는 것
(the noun of “I am learning Korean”)

Now 바로 그 때문이다 can follow each noun to give the referred reason why these actions happen. For example:

제가 가고 싶은 것은 바로 그 때문이에요
That is why I want to go
제가 그녀를 좋아하는 것은 바로 그 때문이에요
That is why I like her
제가 한국어를 배우고 있는 것은 바로 그 때문이에요
That is why I am learning Korean

You can also simply put 그래서 before the action to show what results from a referred situation. As you learned in Lesson 37, 그래서 can refer to a reason from a previous clause. For example:

그래서 가고 싶어요
That is why I want to go (therefore, I want to go)
그래서 그녀를 좋아해요
That is why I like her (therefore, I like her)
그래서 한국어를 배우고 있어요
That is why I am learning Korean (therefore, I am learning Korean)
