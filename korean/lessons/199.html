Introduction

In this lesson you will learn ~은/는커녕, a particle with a lot of meaning packed into it. It stresses a situation to the extreme.

1

~은/는커녕: Far From

~은/는커녕 attaches directly to nouns, or to verbs and adjectives through ~기, giving ~기는커녕. Its nuance is complex, so start with a simple example:

비행기표는커녕 밥도 못 샀어요

Before the translation, break the sentence down. The speaker could not buy food. Food is the first thing anyone with little money buys; it is a basic need. A plane ticket is far down the list. The sentence translates to something like:

= I couldn’t even buy a meal, let alone a plane ticket

Notice the progression. The thing ~은/는커녕 attaches to should be the less likely one. Then you give a more basic thing that did not happen either. If the basic thing did not happen, the less likely thing certainly did not.

In English this often comes out as “far from,” as in this version of the translation:

비행기표는커녕 밥도 못 샀어요
Far from buying a plane ticket, I couldn’t even buy rice

Again, this translation negates the first item and highlights that something even less likely or worse follows.

This is similar to ~조차, which you learned in Lesson 181. But look at the sentence above with ~조차 instead of ~은/는커녕:

비행기표조차 밥도 못 샀어요

This sentence is grammatically illogical. It does not show what the subject failed to do with the plane ticket. If you forced a translation, it would be something like:

Not even the plane ticket, I couldn’t buy a meal either

It sounds illogical because “buying a meal” seems somehow linked to “the plane ticket.”

~은/커녕 gives a sentence a feeling of contrast and comparison. The noun with ~은/는커녕 is the more difficult, extreme or less likely thing. What follows is more expected.
