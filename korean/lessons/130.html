Introduction

In Lesson 10 you first learned Korean numbers, which let you count things, events, minutes, hours, days, months, cars and much more.

Bigger numbers get more complicated. They were not essential early on, but they are important. This lesson introduces larger Korean numbers.

1

Large Korean Numbers

In Lesson 10 you learned that there are two sets of numbers: Pure Korean and Sino-Korean (Chinese). Here we look at large numbers, meaning numbers over 10,000. We can ignore Pure Korean numbers, since in practice they are not used much above sixty or seventy.

Large Sino-Korean numbers are confusing. For English speakers, most of the confusion comes from the fact that “10,000” has its own word in Korean. Here is why.

There is a word for “1” in Korean and English: “일” and “one”.
There is a word for “10” in Korean and English: “십” and “ten”.
There is a word for “100” in Korean and English: “백” and “one hundred”.
There is a word for “1,000” in Korean and English: “천” and “one thousand”.

There is a word for “10,000” in Korean (“만”), but no separate word in English. We just combine “ten” with “thousand” to say “ten thousand.”

When writing out Sino-Korean numbers, everything under 10,000 is one word. For example:

Numbers are spelled out as words in this lesson so you can see how to say them. In real life, numerals would most likely be used for big numbers.

1 = 일
12 = 십이
123 = 백이십삼
1,234 = 천이백삼십사

In English, we count in thousands until we reach one million. For example:

1,000 = one thousand
2,000 = two thousand
45,000 = forty five thousand
134,000 = one hundred thirty four thousand
999,000 = nine hundred ninety nine thousand

In Korean, thousands are counted only up to 10,000. For example:

1,000 = 천
2,000 = 이천
9,000 = 구천
9,999 = 구천구백구십구

At 10,000, the word “만” is used.

10,000 = 만

Large numerals are spaced in units of 10,000. For a number above 9999, the word “만” comes before the rest of the number as a separate word. A simple way to think of it: a space comes after four Arabic digits. For example:

12,345 = 만 이천삼백사십오

Just as we count thousands, Koreans count “ten-thousands.” This is hard for English speakers to get used to. For example:

120,000 = 십이만 – literally “twelve ten-thousands.”
190,000 = 십구만 – literally “nineteen ten-thousands.”

Whatever digits follow the 10,000 unit (that is, after the word “만”) come after a space as the remaining part of the number, if needed. For example:

123,456 = 십이만 삼천사백오십육

We can keep counting “ten-thousands” all the way to “nine-thousand-nine-hundred-ninety-nine ten-thousands.” For example:

1,234,567 = 백이십삼만 사천오백육십칠
Notice that the first word is “one-hundred-twenty-three ten-thousands”

12,345,678 = 천이백삼십사만 오천육백칠십팔
Notice that the first word is “one-thousand-two-hundred-thirty-four ten-thousands”

53,565,453 = 오천삼백오십육만 오천사백오십삼
Notice that the first word is “five-thousand-three-hundred-fifty-six ten-thousands”

99,999,999 = 구천구백구십구만 구천구백구십구
Notice that the first word is “nine-thousand-nine-hundred-ninety-nine ten-thousands”

Remember, spacing in numerals is done in units of 10,000. Now that we have reached “nine-thousand-nine-hundred-ninety-nine ten-thousands,” we add another space in our word.

The number ‘억” means “ten-thousand ten-thousands,” which is a roundabout way of saying “100,000,000” (one hundred million). For example:

100,000,000 = 일억

Just a quick note.

10 is referred to as “십”
100 is referred to as “백”
1,000 is referred to as “천”
10,000 is referred to as “만”

However,

100,000,000 is referred to as “일억.” The numbers above are pronounced without “일”. But for “one” 억, you should say “일억.”

Let’s make more complicated numbers using 억:

123,456,789 = 일억 이천삼백사십오만 육천칠백팔십구
143,549,523 = 일억 사천삼백오십사만 구천오백이십삼

Apartment prices in Korea are usually over one 억, so the word 억 comes up often in real estate advertisements.

Adding more numerals to “억” works just as it did with “만”: the words 천, 백 and 십 tell us how many “units of one-hundred-million” we have. For example:

1,234,567,891 = 십이억 삼천사백오십육만 칠천팔백구십일
Notice that the first word is “twelve one-hundred millions”

12,345,678,912 = 백이십삼억 사천오백육십칠만 팔천구백십이
Notice that the first word is “one-hundred-twenty-three one-hundred millions”

123,456,789,123 = 천이백삼십사억 오천육백칠십팔만 구천백이십삼
Notice that the first word is “one-thousand-two-hundred-thirty-four one-hundred millions”

999,999,999,999 = 구천구백구십구억 구천구백구십구만 구천구백구십구
Notice that the first word is “nine-thousand-nine-hundred-ninety-nine one-hundred millions”

Remember, spacing in numerals is done in units of 10,000. Now that we have reached “nine-thousand-nine-hundred-ninety-nine one-hundred-millions,” we add another space in our word.

The number ‘조” means “ten-thousand one-hundred-millions,” a roundabout way of saying “1,000,000,000,000” (one trillion). For example:

1,000,000,000,000 = 일조

Let’s make more complicated numbers using 조:

1,234,567,891,234 = 일조 이천삼백사십오억 육천칠백팔십구만 천이백삼십사
3,543,454,632,455 = 삼조 오천사백삼십사억 오천사백육십삼만 이천사백오십오

Adding more numerals to “조” works just as with “만” and “억”: the words 천, 백 and 십 tell us how many “units of one-trillion” we have. For example:

12,345,678,912,345 = 십이조 삼천사백오십육억 칠천팔백구십일만 이천삼백사십오
Notice that the first word is “twelve trillions”

123,456,789,123,456 = 백이십삼조 사천오백육십칠억 팔천구백십이만 삼천사백오십육
Notice that the first word is “one-hundred-twenty-three trillions”

1,234,567,891,234,567 = 천이백삼십사조 오천육백칠십팔억 구천백이십삼만 사천오백육십칠
Notice that the first word is “one-thousand—two-hundred-thirty-four trillions”

9,999,999,999,999,999 = 구천구백구십구조 구천구백구십구억 구천구백구십구만 구천구백구십구
Notice that the first word is “nine-thousand—nine-hundred-ninety-nine trillions”

After 만, 억 and 조, the next unit is “경.” 경 is “ten-thousand trillions,” so one “경” is “ten quadrillion.” You rarely meet “경” in everyday life, but it follows the same pattern as 만, 억 and 조: up to 9,999 units can go before 경 before the next unit is needed. For example:

999,900,000,000,000,000 = 구십구경 구천구백조

You might think even “조” would be uncommon, since in English we rarely talk in “trillions.” But the Korean currency (the 원) is roughly 1000:1 against the US dollar, so with money these numbers come up more than you might expect. For example:

Officially, “원” should be separated from the numeral or word by a space, as shown below. Most Korean people leave out the space, so omitting it is acceptable.

1,000 원 (천 원) is roughly $1 and buys a can of pop
10,000 원 (만 원) is roughly $10 and buys a cheap meal
100,000 원 (십만 원) is roughly $100 and buys a cheap cell-phone
1,000,000 원 (백만 원) is roughly $1,000 and buys a nice computer
10,000,000 원 (천만 원) is roughly $10,000 and buys a cheap car
100,000,000 원 (일억 원) is roughly $100,000 and can be a big down-payment on a house
1,000,000,000 원 (십억 원) is roughly $1,000,000 and buys an apartment on the Han River in Seoul.

10,000,000,000 원 (백억 원) is roughly $10,000,000, what top celebrities or athletes make in a year

100,000,000,000 원 (천억 원) is roughly $100,000,000, which might be the revenue of a massive company

1,000,000,000,000 원 (일조 원) is roughly $1,000,000,000 (one billion dollars). Billions of dollars are rare in daily talk, but government debt and spending often run into the “billions of dollars,” so “조” is common in news reports about government spending.
